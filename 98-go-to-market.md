# Go-to-Market: The F-Grade Book

*Working plan, not part of the published book. Drafted 2026-09-10 for Gabriel to cut. Stages and dependencies, no dates.*

## Premise

The F-Grade book is free. The Kickstarter that follows is the revenue event, and it sells what a PDF cannot: the printed book, the marginalia edition, Volatility dice, the poster, personalized pregens, the companion app. The free release therefore has three jobs, in order: get the game played by strangers and hear what happened, build a list of people who want to be told when the Kickstarter opens, and make enough noise that the Kickstarter launches to an audience rather than to a search box.

## Two audiences

**LitRPG readers.** The fans of *Defiance of the Fall*, *Primal Hunter*, and the rest. Large, hungry for more of the thing, and mostly people who have never sat at a tabletop RPG. The hook is being Zack. The System is an AI in their fiction, so a game co-designed with one reads as on-theme rather than as a scandal. Where they are: r/litrpg, r/ProgressionFantasy, Royal Road, the series' own Discords and subreddits, the LitRPG Facebook groups, LitRPG podcasts and BookTube channels, Audible listener communities.

**Tabletop RPG players.** They know what a session is and will judge the rules on the rules. They are the people who will actually run it for a table of readers. Where they are: r/rpg, r/rpgdesign, RPGGeek, the itch.io tabletop community, DriveThruRPG, ENWorld, RPG podcasts and actual-play channels. In these spaces AI-generated art and AI co-authorship are hostile topics, and both major storefronts require disclosure.

**Readers first.** They are the bigger pool and the underserved one, and RPG players find the game through readers who ask "how do I play this." Two consequences the plan has to own: the book needs a first-time-RPG on-ramp, which is product work rather than marketing, and the AI disclosure is stated plainly everywhere and defended nowhere. The "How This Book Was Written" front matter is the disclosure and the story at the same time.

## What "capitalizing on users" means here

A player who finishes the tutorial is worth four things, and each needs an ask at the moment the value lands:

1. **A report.** The playtest report form, linked from the last page of the book and from the GM screen. The Session-End Sweep is already the shape of a good report (the three biggest moments, what they shared); the form asks for those, plus levels reached, what dragged, what broke, and whether they would run it again.
2. **A referral.** The Phase 6 individual summary is the natural share: a card a player wants to show someone. A line under it points at the download.
3. **A follower.** The mailing list and the Kickstarter pre-launch page. The list is owned; storefront followers are rented.
4. **A beta tester.** The companion app waitlist, opened whether or not the app exists at release. Whatever GMs find tedious to log by hand is the app's feature list, so the waitlist form asks that question.

## Stages

**Stage 0: the surface exists before anyone hears the name.**

- Domains registered (gradebreaker.games as the site; the .com and gradebreakerrpg.com redirect).
- A landing page: what it is in one paragraph, the download, the mailing list, the Discord, the report form. Nothing else.
- Storefront listings on itch.io (pay-what-you-want, devlog, community tab) and DriveThruRPG (discoverability), both pointing back to the site. Check each storefront's AI-content policy before listing and follow it to the letter.
- A Discord server with four channels: playtest reports, rules questions, looking for group, and the design diary feed. Moderation load is Gabriel's until it is not.
- The report form and the app waitlist form, both linked from inside the book.
- A press and creator kit: the one-paragraph pitch, the cover, three spreads, the transparency story, and a short note on how to get a review copy (it is free; the note is about who to talk to).
- Three to five outside GMs recruited to run the tutorial cold before launch. Their reports are the cold-read test the design cycle has been aiming at since 2026-07-08, and their quotes are launch content.

Blocks: domains, license decision, storefront policy check, the report form's questions.

**Stage 1: launch.**

- Announce in reader spaces first, tabletop spaces second, a few days apart, so the first wave of comments comes from people who want the thing to exist.
- Design diaries are the content engine. The project has an unusually complete design history in the backlog and the git log: the Hidden Vector Engine, Yield and the lethality math, the flat-VE decision, the tutorial crisis and what the modules taught. One diary per week is sustainable; each one is a reason to mention the game again.
- One actual play of the tutorial, recorded, by someone who is not Gabriel if possible. Readers who have never played need to see a session before they will run one.

Blocks: Stage 0 complete, the release build proofread.

**Stage 2: iterate in public.**

- Versioned releases (0.1, 0.2) with changelogs. Each version is an announcement, and "here is what changed because of your reports" is the most persuasive post a free game can make.
- Feedback triage into the backlog on a stated cadence, with the triage itself visible.
- A community content policy once the license allows it: pregens, one-shots, alternate valleys. The first fan-made thing is a milestone worth celebrating out loud.

**Stage 3: momentum to the Kickstarter.**

- The trigger for launching is a number, not a date: a mailing-list size and a count of returned playtest reports that Gabriel sets in advance.
- The pre-launch page opens when Stage 2 has produced at least one versioned update, so followers arrive to a project that visibly moves.
- The app beta, if it exists, is the wedge: it is the thing readers of the free PDF cannot get any other way.
- The physical goods are the Kickstarter's reason to exist, since the PDF stays free.

## Open decisions

- **License.** Creative Commons on the rules text (BY-NC-SA keeps commercial rights and invites fan content; BY is the open-content play; a proprietary license with a fan policy is the conservative one), with the art excluded in every case. This decision gates Stage 2's community content and should be made before Stage 0.
- **Disclosure wording.** Where the AI statement appears on each storefront and on the site, and whether it leads or follows the pitch. Recommendation: it leads on the site and in the front matter, and appears wherever a storefront asks.
- **The app at launch.** Decided 2026-09-10: some version of the app ships with the release, so the pitch mentions it and the waitlist becomes the beta sign-up.
- **Who answers the Discord.** Support is a job; decide how much of it Gabriel does.
- **Solo mode.** A cheap way for a reader with no group to play at all, and possibly the best conversion tool for the reader audience. Listed as Maybe in the backlog; the reader-first strategy raises its priority.
