# The Principle System

## What a Principle Is

Principle is the fundamental fabric of reality: compressed patterns of how things behave, discovered and formalized by the System. A Principle is not a spell list or an element. It is an operational truth: Fire consumes and spreads. Sharpness concentrates force into a decisive boundary. Space defines distance and adjacency. A character who internalizes such a truth can apply it.

Every Principle is organized around a **Concept**, the pattern's name and identity. Concepts range from the elemental (Fire, Earth, Lightning) to the abstract (Edge, Flow, Pressure, Consumption, Resonance). All valid Concepts share three properties:

- **Operational:** the Concept does something specific.
- **Bounded:** it does not apply everywhere.
- **Testable:** its use produces observable outcomes.

If a proposed Concept cannot meet all three, it is not a Principle; it is a mood. The GM enforces this at Compression (below).

**What a Principle grants.** A Principle does not raise stats. Across its tiers it grants:

- **Applications:** active techniques, in combat and out, costing Beats and Aether (Searing Strike, Spatial Step).
- **Passives:** resistances and affinities aligned to the Concept.
- **Infusion:** at Mid Fragment, the Concept woven into your ordinary actions at no Beat or Aether cost.
- **A Domain:** at Peak Fragment, a localized zone of systemic control.
- **Narrative permissions:** things you simply can do: no roll, no cost.

## Concept Slots

A character holds a limited number of Principle Concepts:

| **Grade** | **Concept Slots** |
|---|---|
| F-Grade | 1 |
| E-Grade | 2 |
| D-Grade | 3 |
| C-Grade | 4 |

Each Grade Breakthrough grants one new slot. These are the "new Principle slots" awarded at Recognition (see Grade Breakthroughs). A slot, once filled, holds its Concept for life; the only ways a Concept leaves a slot are Refinement (it changes shape) and Fusion (it merges with another).

## Insight Points

Progression within a Concept is measured in **Insight Points (IP)**. IP totals are visible; the System reports them the way it reports levels:

::: systemvoice
**[Insight Gained: Weight 8/10]**
*The mountain does not strike. It arrives.*
:::

The number is public to the character; what the System is forging from it is not.

**Earning IP.** IP accrues only from experiences aligned with the Concept. A character cannot grind generic experience into Sharpness insight: they must cut, be cut, study cutting, survive the edge.

| **Source** | **IP Awarded** |
|---|---|
| Battle Memory meditation | 1–3, by the memory's intensity |
| Consuming an affinity treasure | 1–2 |
| Consolidation vision | 1 |
| Surviving a life-or-death situation through the Concept | 2 |

**Battle Memories** are the primary pipeline from play to insight. In moments of extreme stress (surviving at near-zero HP, witnessing something beyond comprehension, achieving an outcome the System deems statistically improbable), the GM grants a **Battle Memory Card**. Two triggers are automatic: a Volatility cascade of two or more extra dice on a player character's roll, and surviving being Downed (see Core Mechanics, "Downed and Death"). During the next Consolidation, the player describes how they meditate on it: what they felt, what they noticed, what pattern they think they glimpsed. The System AI returns a cryptic vision and awards IP toward the Concept the memory most closely expresses; the vision procedure for every run mode is in The System AI chapter. A character who fights but never reflects grows in raw stats and stagnates in Principle; a character who reflects deeply on fewer, more intense experiences can outpace one who grinds.

## The Progression Ladder

| **Tier** | **Cumulative IP** | **Grants** |
|---|---|---|
| Initial Insight | 3 | Minor passive (e.g., +5% Fire Resistance) |
| Seed | 10 | First Application; narrative permissions |
| Early Fragment | 25 | Second Application; passive doubles |
| Mid Fragment | 50 | Infusion |
| Peak Fragment | 100 | Domain |

**Tier caps by Grade.** A Concept's tier cannot exceed the cap for the character's current Grade:

| **Grade** | **Tier Cap** |
|---|---|
| F-Grade | Seed |
| E-Grade | Mid Fragment |
| D-Grade | Peak Fragment |

IP continues to accrue past a capped threshold, but Compression is impossible until Breakthrough lifts the cap. A character who Breaks Through with banked IP may Compress at their next Consolidation. Tiers beyond Peak Fragment exist at C-Grade and above; their development is deferred alongside the higher-Grade Breakthrough content.

**Worked progression:** Kara, an F-Grade brawler, fights heavy and direct. Over six sessions she banks two Battle Memories (2 IP, 3 IP), survives a cave-in by holding a falling slab (2 IP), and receives one Consolidation vision (1 IP): 8 IP toward **Weight**. Two sessions later a third Battle Memory (2 IP) carries her to 10. At her next Consolidation she Compresses, articulates the pattern ("things fall the way I decide"), and unlocks her Seed Application. She is Level 6 with POW 18; the 10-Aether cost is heavy but castable, exactly once per fight with room to spare.

## Compression

Meeting an IP threshold does not advance the tier. Advancement requires **Compression**, a declared act during Consolidation.

The GM asks: *"What pattern have you discovered in how you act, or in how the world behaves?"*

The player answers from their character's lived experience. The GM and System AI then work with the player to refine the articulation into the tier's mechanical expression: enforcing operational clarity, defining scope and limits, and assigning the Application, passive, or Domain it produces. Refinement is collaborative and continues until the articulation is Operational, Bounded, and Testable; the expression that results is full strength however plainly it was first spoken. What shapes the expression is the pattern the character has actually lived, which matters most at Peak Fragment, where the articulation determines the Domain's character.

**Refinement.** The articulation can steer an existing Concept rather than deepen it as-is. A character whose Fire has grown hungrier with every fight may Compress Fire into **Consuming Flame**: same slot, same tier, same IP, shifted expression. The System AI adjusts the Concept's Applications and permissions to match. Refinement is how a Principle stays true to the person wielding it as the Hidden Vector Engine's read of them evolves.

**New Concepts enter at the bottom.** Mastery is per-Concept and never transfers. A C-Grade fire cultivator who turns to Sharpness starts at 0 IP and climbs to Initial Insight and Seed like any novice. Two costs keep late acquisition honest:

- The new Concept's Applications are priced at their **acquisition Grade** (see Core Mechanics, "Aether Costs Are Set at Acquisition") and carry that Grade's potency envelope (see "Application Grade" below). A Seed acquired at C-Grade costs 10,000 Aether per use and manifests at C-Grade scale.
- Its IP must come from experiences aligned to the *new* Concept: attention and risk spent there instead of deepening what the character already holds.

A late Concept is narrow rather than weak: one Application, full price, full scale.

## Activation Costs

| **Tier** | **Beat Cost** | **Aether Cost (F-Grade origin)** |
|---|---|---|
| Seed Application | 1 Beat | 10 |
| Early Fragment Application | 1 Beat | 15 |
| Mid Fragment Infusion | Free | Free |
| Peak Fragment Domain | 1 Beat to activate | 30 + 5/round |

Aether costs scale ×10 per Grade of the Application's origin and are fixed to that origin forever; a Seed learned at F-Grade costs 10 Aether for the rest of the character's life. See Core Mechanics, "The Aether System," for the full table and the attrition dynamics this creates.

**Principle and Spells:** A character's Principle passives always apply to matching spells automatically. Using an active Application alongside a spell requires Infusion tier. Below that, choose one per Beat: cast the spell or use the Application.

## Application Grade

Every Application has a Grade of its own, set at the moment it is acquired. That Grade fixes two things for the Application's whole existence: its Aether cost, and its **potency envelope**: the scale of world the manifestation can touch. The Clash bonus stays inside the Modifier Budget at every Grade; what grows is everything around the number.

**Searing Strike at three acquisition Grades** (the Seed Application of a Fire Concept):

- **F-Grade (10 Aether):** +10 to a Clash against targets vulnerable to fire. The flame wraps the weapon's edge, ignites cloth and dry wood, and leaves scorch lines on flesh.
- **E-Grade (100 Aether):** the same +10. The flame runs white and dense: it chars through leather, ruins the temper of a parried blade, and the wounds it leaves cauterize shut, still smoking.
- **D-Grade (1,000 Aether):** the same +10. The strike arrives as a sheet of fire with an edge in it. It passes through a steel door as through bread, and the Zone smells of ozone for hours.

The envelope is narrative permission scaled by Grade. When an envelope collides with the world (can it burn this? can that survive it?), the GM reads the Application's Grade against the obstacle's Grade the same way any Cross-Grade question resolves.

**Re-forging.** During any Compression, a character may also re-forge one Application they already know at their current Grade. The Application takes its new Grade's cost and envelope, and the old version is gone. An F-forged Searing Strike stays 10 Aether forever, a lit match that a D-Grade warrior can flick all day; re-forged at D-Grade it becomes a door-cutter that costs 1,000 a swing. Keeping the cheap old trick or paying full price for real power is a genuine choice, made one Application at a time.

## Narrative Permissions

From Seed tier, a Concept grants permissions, capabilities that need no roll, Beat, or Aether. Baseline for every Concept:

- You **sense** the Concept's presence and behavior nearby (flame, edges, spatial distortion).
- **Mundane interactions** with the Concept stop requiring rolls (lighting a fire, splitting kindling clean, judging a distance exactly).
- You are **comfortable in environments** the Concept dominates (extreme heat, knife-storms of debris, warped space).

Specific permissions beyond the baseline are generated by the GM and System AI to fit the Concept and deepen as tiers rise. Permissions are fictional capability, not combat mechanics. If a use would demand a Clash roll, it is an Application, not a permission.

## Principle Fusion

A character holding **two Concepts at Seed tier or higher** may attempt to fuse them during a Compression. Fusion is a Resistance Roll:

> **d100 + PER Force vs. Severe (140) of the character's Grade**

- **Success:** the two Concepts merge into a single fused Concept (Earth + Fire become **Magma**, Edge + Flow become **Severance**) at the **lower parent's tier**, occupying **one slot**. The System AI generates bespoke Applications for it. The freed slot is empty and may later take a new Concept, entering at the bottom like any other.
- **Failure:** the patterns refuse unification. The higher-tier parent loses 10 IP, and the fusion cannot be reattempted until after the character's next Breakthrough.

Fused Concepts are more powerful and less stable than their parents: their Applications price at the peak of the Modifier Budget (+15 to +20), and any natural 01–05 rolled while using one resolves as a Catastrophic Failure.

## Design Intent

Principles are discovered, not chosen, because the discovery *is* the behavioral signal. What a character does under pressure, recorded by the Hidden Vector Engine, determines which Concepts the System offers, which Battle Memories resonate, and what a Compression articulation can honestly claim. The axis-by-axis Concept affinities are documented in the Hidden Vector Engine chapter.

Slots are scarce and entry is always at the bottom so that a Principle is a biography, not a shopping list. A C-Grade character's four Concepts, their Refinements, and any Fusions between them should read as the mechanical transcript of who that character has been.

## The System and Principles

The System exists to discover and refine stable patterns of reality that cannot be derived directly. Reality is not fixed: zones shift, laws degrade, unknown conditions emerge faster than any static model can solve. So the System introduces conscious agents into constrained environments and observes: conflict reveals limits, scarcity reveals priorities, uncertainty reveals models, pressure reveals identity.

Patterns that succeed across contexts are compressed, formalized, and made reusable. That is a Principle, and that is why the System pressures its holders: dominant strategies are challenged, over-reliance is punished, and no single approach remains optimal forever. The System does not ask "Did you win?" It asks "How do you win, and what does that reveal?"

Power is not granted. It is recognized, compressed, and returned to those who discovered it.
