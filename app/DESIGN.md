# Companion App Design

The app listens to an online session, logs what happened, keeps the HVE record, and offers the GM real-time suggestions for quests, titles, and classes. Around those four features it keeps the campaign's game record: characters, combat, Consolidation, advancement, and the System's notices to each player.

Sources, in order of authority: the book (`book/`) for procedures, `rules/*.yaml` for numbers, then this file. The ChatGPT handoff spec (2026-09-25) was input to this file; where the two differ, this file holds. A question the book does not answer goes into the book first (the RulesGap rule in `rules/README.md`), then into the app.

## Decisions

Ruled by Gabriel, 2026-09-25.

- **The core is listening, event logging, HVE logging, and live suggestions.** Rules answers are a convenience and sit at the bottom of the list.
- **First target: the tutorial, played online with Gabriel's group, after the app listens and logs.** In-person listening at a physical table stays an idea for later.
- **The GM is the captain.** AI observes, drafts, and proposes. Every AI-assisted action has a manual control that produces the same record.
- **What reaches a player needs the GM's tap.** Bookkeeping only the GM's tracker shows (start the round, apply damage, run a Consolidation) executes at once with undo. Awards, notices, titles, and anything else a player would see wait for one-tap confirmation, whether they came from speech, a suggestion, or a counter.
- **Dice are rolled in the app.** The engine reads the natural die, so explosions grant Marks and cascades grant Battle Memory Cards without anyone reporting them. The GM can roll privately. Typed-in results stay available and record the natural die and the extra dice.
- **Zones live in the combat tracker** as named buckets with each combatant in one. No map.
- **What Can Be Seen filters every player view,** including anything sent to a player-facing AI. Players wear or hide Bestowed titles from their own interface. Open HVE logs are a campaign option, off by default (Introduction, "Who Reads What").
- **Consolidation goals are table talk.** The GM enters hours per character; the app computes and previews.
- **The GM has one composer for System messages:** to one player, several, or the party; sent now or held; typed, or drafted by AI in the System's voice. The engine's automatic notices go through the same channel.
- **Campaigns pin a rules version** and migrate when the GM says so. Amended by Gabriel, 2026-09-27: until the first printed edition every campaign follows the current rules; from then, campaigns pin an edition (see "Rules editions").
- **VE from a kill is shared** by every participant; a confirmed kill for the Slaughter titles is the finishing blow (Titles, "Achievement Titles"). With in-app dice, the finishing blow is whoever made the attack that took the creature to 0.
- **"Once a day" resets at dawn** in the fiction, on the app's in-game clock. The book states the dawn-to-dawn rule (rules 0.1.22); Gabriel accepted dawn at 06:00 (2026-09-27; rules 0.1.23), and the sentence is queued for the book.

Ruled by Gabriel, 2026-09-26:

- **The notices' wording stands** as written in `packages/web/src/text.ts` (`noticeLine`); a test holds it to in-world words.
- **A correction changes a player's sheet silently.** No notice explains it; the GM explains at the table.

Also ruled by Gabriel, 2026-09-26 (characters and screens):

- **A character lives in one campaign.** The campaign's log is its record; it never belongs to two campaigns and never leaves one.
- **Players build characters, and so can the GM.** A player creates a character in a campaign they play in, or builds one outside any campaign and assigns it to a campaign later; assigning moves it into the campaign's log. The GM can still create a character and assign it to a player, and can reassign any character (a player leaves, the GM takes the character over). A character built outside a campaign is checked against the campaign's rules when it joins.
- **The player-choice rule.** A player records the choices the book gives to the player: spending free points, accepting one of the three class offers, choosing the Consolidation that meditates on a Battle Memory, wearing or hiding a Bestowed title, accepting an articulation on the Quiet Path, creating their own character. The GM records everything adjudicated: awards, outcomes, assigned points, offers, corrections. Combat declarations (Yield, Surge, a pill) are the player's too; whether players tap them on their own screens is settled with the combat tracker.
- **"Assigned points"** is the name for the three Attribute points the System assigns each level (the book's rename is queued). Free points are the player's.
- **Roles belong to a campaign,** never to an account: the GM screen and the player's interface differ by the role held in that campaign. The GM screen has sections, and the GM can view any player's screen as that player sees it.
- **Every GM action shows what it will record** under "If you record this", and its button names the action. The preview is redone whenever the log moves, so a form left open while something else is recorded shows what it would do now.

Ruled by Gabriel, 2026-09-26 (the party and the composer):

- **The notice feed is rebuilt from the log.** Engine notices are a view of the record and the GM's System messages are actions in it, so a reload shows the same feed an open page does. A notice from an action later undone or corrected leaves the feed on every screen at once. This replaces the earlier "notices are not stored".
- **The party is part of the record.** One character invites another, and the invitee's player accepts or declines on their own screen; the GM can record either for a player away from theirs. Accepting joins the inviter's party, or forms one of the two. A character is in one party at a time and leaves before joining another; a party left with one member disbands; the GM disbands. The party frame shows each member's Health, Maximum Health, Aether, and Downed, and nothing else (What Can Be Seen). A player can invite the characters other players hold; characters the GM holds are left off that list. Quest sharing arrives with quests; membership ending at death arrives with the combat tracker.
- **The composer addresses characters.** The System speaks to a character, so a message goes to characters; "the party" is expanded to its members when the GM sends. A held message reaches nobody until the GM sends it, and undoing it discards it. The composer warns on the table's words (`rules/system-ai.yaml`, `voice.units`) and leaves the call to the GM.
- **Rolls are logged, and the server rolls them.** A roll is an action carrying its dice, so replay reads the same dice and no browser can supply its own; dice the table rolled by hand are typed in by the GM and marked. A player rolls openly for their own characters and cannot take a roll back; the GM rolls for anyone, privately if they choose, and can undo a roll made by mistake. Dice are table talk: they sit in their own panel beside the System's notices, and a player sees every open roll without the GM's Resistance or the outcome. The roll button is the roll, with no preview.
- **Players drive their own characters in a fight** (Gabriel, 2026-09-26, under the player-choice rule): from their own screen a player takes their character's activation, attacks, moves, finishes, defends, and Yields. The GM can record any of it and holds Undo; forced moves, free strikes, Cornered, and Exposed from the fiction are the GM's. Flanking is offered on every attack, pre-checked when another hostile shares the target's Zone.
- **Dice come before the combat tracker,** and the tracker is built in slices, each usable at the table: sides, Momentum, rounds, Beats, and HP; the Clash with the Rule of 40, Yield, Exposed, Flanking, and Advantage; Downed, pills, Aura Pressure, and the Will Save; encounter end.
- **Slice 3's rulings** (Gabriel, 2026-09-26; book sentences queued as backlog edits 7 to 10): pills count per Consolidation, 5 per kind, every pill in a fight or out of one, with no per-fight limit (a pill outside a fight is `pill.take`; the count resets at the first full hour of Consolidation); a defender may Yield against a Surprise Beat; the round of Downing is the first of the three; bare-hands stabilizing needs the same Zone and is a DEX check (edit 11).
- **Claude's calls in titles, all accepted by Gabriel (2026-09-26):** nothing is conferred without the GM, and passing on a due title removes it from the due list for good (the Titles tab can still confer it); first blood is the first damage a Clash deals in a fight; "still standing" is read at the fight's end, before a stabilized character wakes at 1 HP; Deep Breather counts rests with at least one full hour, and a collapse's involuntary Consolidation does not count; a negative stat bonus stops at 0; "Title echoed" and "Title released" are drafts for the voice pass ("Title conferred" is the book's).
- **Claude's calls in quests, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-26):** a Routine, Faction, or Personal Opportunity quest is offered to one character, and a Routine or Faction quest then reaches the party by sharing; a character's refusal of a Mandate leaves it binding on the others; when a party ends, its shared quests stay with their holders, unshared, and sharing again multiplies again; every participating holder collects the quest's stated award and a holder above the quest's Grade collects nothing (Gabriel's ruling, book edit 16), and the GM can set any holder's amount; Expire closes a quest silently with no refusal counted; a quest is keyed by the action that issued it and its code may repeat, so the same code goes to several characters as separate quests (2026-09-27, for the tutorial's Q-001); the GM may complete a quest whose count is short; a Hidden quest's holder sees neither its code nor its count until it completes.
- **The System shows the owner their items** (Gabriel, 2026-09-26; book edit 14): the interface lists what the character carries after Proficiencies, an item gained brings an "Item registered" notice, and nobody else sees another character's items. An attack starts with the shape the character has the most Marks in; a defense starts with none.
- **Claude's calls in encounter end, all accepted by Gabriel (2026-09-26):** a kill is a combatant who died (a Downed NPC left alive released no VE); every participant gets a "Kill confirmed" notice for each kill and one notice for the VE total, so an override never contradicts a per-kill number; the boss's 1.5× rounds down, as the engine rounds every award (book edit 13); a loot chance succeeds on a d100 at or under it (book edit 12); the loot is rolled once, and rolling again means undoing the roll; players divide the spoils themselves under the player-choice rule, one item per click; a character's pill comes from what they carry, and a creature's or NPC's is the GM's to say.
- **Claude's calls in slice 3, all five accepted by Gabriel (2026-09-26):** a fight with a character still dying cannot end (the GM stabilizes them, rules their fate, or runs the rounds out); the Will Save is rolled for characters, and a creature's or NPC's Suppression is the GM's toggle; resistance from a save lasts the encounter whichever higher-Grade entity is present; a Downed character makes the presence save when they wake; a pill that does nothing still costs its Beat and counts toward the recipient's two.

- **Claude's calls in Battle Memories and Principles (2026-09-27):** a card the rules make due reaches the player only when the GM grants it and writes it; a cascade's card cannot be withheld and a Downing's can; a cascade counts on every character roll the record knows (the dice tab, a Clash, a check, Momentum, Seize); the player marks the card to meditate on, and the GM may still record a meditation on an unmarked one; a meditation needs a Consolidation completed after the card arrived; the vision reaches the player as written, with nothing added; Insight keeps accruing while a crystallized family waits on its name; one Distillation takes one tier, so two thresholds met take two, with no limit per Consolidation; a Quiet Path veto discards the offer with no penalty and the GM may offer again; "Battle Memory retained", "Articulation proposed", "Distillation complete", and "Principle refined" are drafts for the voice pass ("Resonance accruing", "Insight Gained", and "Initial Insight" are the book's). Gabriel accepted these calls (2026-09-27) and ruled that no rule counts sessions: the Consolidation vision's limit is once a day in the app until the book rules.
- **Class techniques in the tracker (Gabriel accepted the shape, 2026-09-27; Claude's calls within it open to his cut):** the app pays every technique's cost from its cost type: 5 Aether at F (×10 per Grade the class was acquired at), once per fight (marked on the combatant), or 10 Health or Exposed (a technique names its drawback, or the player chooses; outside a fight only Health); a technique may carry one hook the app applies, a Clash bonus on a named side or a heal with a reach, and the four book techniques that fit carry theirs in the data (Triage, Breach, Pay in Blood, Sworn Blow); a technique that shapes a Clash is declared with it (a checkbox on the attack or defense, one roll, no extra Beat), and one without a Clash hook is also offered as a use on its own, which spends a Beat unless the technique takes none; a heal's targets are the character's own side within reach, and "the next Zone" is not checked because the Zones carry no map; a heal wakes a Downed ally, as any healing does; outside a fight an Aether or Drawback technique can be used from the class section, and a once-per-fight one cannot; the effect of a technique without a hook stays the GM's; permissions that bend the action economy stay by hand; the full ability grammar is built with M2's class-offer drafting.
- **Claude's calls in Prep (2026-09-27; Gabriel accepted the log and the filter):** prepared items live in the campaign's log as the GM's own actions (`prep.save`, `prep.remove`), so they persist, reach every GM screen live, undo like anything else, and never reach a player; the Campaign log section hides them, and undos of them, unless the GM shows them; a pack loads as one save, so one undo removes it, and loading it again replaces its items with the book's; firing records the real action (a message, a quest's issue, a fight's start) with `prep:<id>` as its cause, Prep counts what has fired from those causes, and the item stays until the GM removes it; a notice fires from Prep with its text editable for the counts play fills in, to the characters the GM checks (every player's character by default); a fight opens in Combat's setup and a quest in the Quests form, filled in, so placing characters and picking holders happen where they always do; a fight saves to Prep from setup and a quest from the issue form; the tutorial pack holds the notices the GM delivers by hand and leaves out the ones the record emits (a kill confirmed, a technique acquired, a title conferred); Q-001, which the book gives to each character separately, fires once per character, each holding their own quest under the book's code (quests are keyed by the action that issued them, and a code may repeat).
- **Claude's calls in the Bestiary and encounter sizing, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-27):** the Bestiary is a GM section (the sizing table with the party's row and every stat block, with the VE its kill pays and the Force it counts at from Level 8), and fights are still built in Combat, where a sizing reading sits under the creatures chosen; the party is the characters placed in the fight and the creatures are everyone on a side without a character; the party level is the average level rounded down, and the GM can type another (queued for the book); a creature counts at the higher of all its Offense and Defense lines, conditional ones included; a Force between two columns reads as the harder one, and under the easy column reads as easier than easy; the table's reading stops, and says why, for a creature of another Grade, a creature entered by hand (no tier or Forces), and a set of tiers that is no cell of the row; a creature with other than two Beats is sized by its Force with a note that the rows assume two. From Level 8 any number of creatures is sized, 10 Force each past the second (rules 0.1.35); five or more carry a note that the book tested the step to four.
- **Claude's calls in inspection, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-27):** inspection is a read, never a record: a glance costs nothing and changes nothing, so it is not logged; a player's character can inspect every other living character a player holds and everyone still in the running fight, the fiction deciding what is in sight; the server sends each read already filtered by the Grade gap, so a hidden title never reaches the browser of someone who cannot read it; only active titles are read, Echoed and released ones being history (queued for the book); a creature or NPC a Grade or more above does not resolve, and one that resolves shows its titles (a creature holds none); the GM's Inspection tab reads any character from another character or from an NPC or creature of a chosen Grade, and lists what that gap leaves unread.
- **Claude's calls in classes, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-27):** the GM records the three offers together, once the character is Level 10 or above with no class, and they reach the player at once; the offers stand until one is accepted, and writing others means undoing them; an offer is one of the book's classes as written (their notices live in `rules/classes.yaml`, held to the book's boxes by the prose lint) or written fresh; the numbers block (a profile's points match its shape, the technique takes one of the three costs) and the book's advice warns without blocking (more than one action-economy effect, a second guarded class in the campaign, the table's words in the notice); the player accepts on their own screen with a confirming step, the GM can record the choice for a player away, and an accepted class stands (the GM alone can undo one recorded by mistake); the selection bonus lands first, then the profile places every held assigned point from Level 10, and each later level's at the level; assigned points bound for a stat at the cap are lost, the GM's before Level 10 and the profile's after, and so is selection bonus past it (Gabriel's ruling, 2026-09-27: nothing redirects them; queued for the book); a once-a-day permission is marked in the data (Finder's Share, Standing), the player or the GM records each use, and it is ready again at the next dawn on the clock, with no limit kept without the clock; the offers and the class reach only the holder's player, without the guarded mark or the book's name; the Level 10 notice is one notice (the opening line, then each offer), and "Class accepted" is a draft for the voice pass.
- **Claude's calls in the in-game clock, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-27):** the clock counts minutes from Day 1, 00:00, Day 1 being the day of Integration; the day's number changes at midnight and once-a-day uses reset at dawn; dawn is 06:00 (`dawn_hour`, queued for the book) unless the GM sets another hour with the clock; days survived is the day's number less one, raised for every living character each time the clock moves, and the GM can still tick it; a quest's limit in hours runs from the moment it is issued and needs the clock set; passing a limit tells the GM and leaves the quest open, since failing or expiring it is the GM's call and a player sees the result; the holder's log shows the hours left rounded up, never the clock; an hour forward and forward to dawn record at once, while setting the clock and a custom span show their preview; setting the clock backward is allowed, as a correction, and passes no dawns.
- **Claude's calls in sessions, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-27):** attendance is by character, since the record is about characters (a player's absence is their characters' absence, and a GM-run character is marked present); one session runs at a time and a new one cannot start until the last ends; ending a session without a sweep is allowed, with a warning and a link to the sweep; the sweep still offers every entry not yet swept, whichever session logged it, so a missed sweep carries forward; attendance changes record at once, as GM-only bookkeeping, while start and end show their preview; the summary can be written or rewritten after the session ends.
- **Claude's calls in events, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-27):** an event is one thing that happened and carries at most one HVE entry per character, the book's structured entry split into the event's shared fields (summary, context) and each character's own (side, intensity, intent, outcome); an event's id is the id of the action that logs it; entries are drafts for the sweep, never tallies on their own, so the hand sweep stays the one place Current and Deep change; an entry at 1 or more is offered into the sweep and a 0.5 entry is offered unchecked, counting 1 when raised; a Defining entry's margin note is the event's summary; the GM may log a 0.5 entry by hand (the book reserves the tier for the app, and the GM's hand entry is the app's manual path); events are the GM's alone.
- **Claude's calls in the HVE sweep, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-27):** the sweep is one action covering every character with a moment, so undoing it undoes the session's sweep; a session where nothing comes to mind records nothing; a note may ride on any moment as a cue, and only a Defining moment requires one; a moment of 1 tally carries no secondary (one weight lower is none); a dead character is not swept; copying Deep across sets all eight sides at once; the sweep in progress is kept in the GM's browser until it is recorded; the party-wide count past the expected 3 to 6 is a warning, never a block.

- **Claude's calls in the provider adapter, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-28):** a key the provider refuses is not stored, and one the provider could not be asked about (limited, unreachable) is stored with the problem shown; the key is sealed with a server secret rather than kept in plain text, since a database copy should not carry working keys, and a changed secret means GMs enter keys again; the GM sees the key's last four characters and never the rest, so a key is replaced, never read back; usage is shown in tokens, and dollars stay on the provider's console, whose prices change; one feature name per request ("draft-events", "sweep") so usage reads by feature; the card lives in the Table section with the campaign's other setup rather than as its own section.
- **Notices a player's own action sends (Claude's call, accepted by Gabriel, 2026-09-27):** two notices reach a player without the GM's tap: a player's own exploding roll earns a Mark and brings "Technique acquired" or its advance, and a player's attack that Downs another player's character brings that character "Vital coherence: 3". Both stand: the roll is the dice, which the table sees when they land (Dice are rolled in the app), and the Downing is the fight's state; the GM's Undo takes either back, with its notice. The tap rule governs what the GM awards, confers, or sends.
- **Claude's calls in event drafting, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-28):** the drafter sees no HVE reading of any character (no Deep, no Current, no pole of an earlier entry; earlier events by summary only), so an entry comes from what the character did in the lines (The Hidden Vector Engine, "Do not steer"); the instructions say a wrong draft costs the GM more than a miss, since a missed moment can still be logged by hand; every drafted event carries at least one entry, and one without is dropped, since the tracker already holds what happened in a fight; the 0.5 tier is described to the model as a real choice that falls short of a full tally because it cost little or passed quickly (the book says only "below the sweep's threshold"); each entry's one-sentence reason is shown to the GM and not stored in the record, whose notes are the GM's own; a scene goes as one request until live play needs windows of lines; an expected event at 0.5 is optional in a script (Gabriel left the call to Claude, 2026-09-28), since a reminder sits below the sweep's threshold and the GM may raise it, so finding one counts and missing one costs nothing.
- **Claude's calls in drafting the bookkeeping, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-28):** a second request with its own instructions, so the measured events prompt stays as it is and each is judged on its own numbers; both run side by side on every draft, and one failing leaves the other's drafts with the failure said; a thing found goes to the character who took it, or to the spoils when the GM lists it and a character claims a piece afterward (a give, then a move), and an item taken from someone outside the party is a give; water, a meal eaten on the spot, and a Core absorbed the moment it is held are not items; each change of hands is its own draft, except a thing passing through someone's hands on a shared line, which is one; a quest's completion awards each holder its stated VE (0 for a scaled quest), which the GM edits on the card; a Prep cue is a pointer, fired from Prep, and counts as settled once its item fires after the draft; VE awards and party invitations followed the same day: the drafter grants VE only when the GM states it aloud and no other record pays it (a Core absorbed by name, or other with a note), never for a kill, a quest's completion, or a rest, and to the characters the grant covers; an answer names its invitation by who sent it to whom, one waiting in the record or one drafted before it, and accepting it waits until the GM has accepted that invitation, then names it as recorded. The suggestion panel's kinds wait for the next pass.
- **Claude's calls in the suggestion panel, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-28):** the panel is its own GM section, second in the bar, with a count of what waits; it holds everything waiting on the GM: from the record, the catalog titles whose count is met and the Battle Memory Cards the rules make due (the same cards the Party and Principles sections show), with pointers to the section that settles anything else due (assigned points, class offers, a Principle to crystallize or distill, a quest whose time ran out); from table talk, the drafter's suggestions and the Prep cues, which move out of the Events review so drafting stays in Events and reviewing what the fiction earned happens here. A third request drafts the suggestions, with its own instructions, so each drafter is judged on its own numbers: the titles no count tracks (the tutorial's, each by its deed), Battle Memory Cards the GM grants for extreme stress (never the automatic ones, which the record makes due, and the cards already due are in the request), and Hidden Achievements written in the book's three parts (name, the deed as trigger, +3 to +5 to one Attribute, read from `rules/titles.yaml`). Each suggestion carries the grant accepting it records (`title.grant` or `memory.grant`), editable before the GM confers, and is accepted only for the character it names. A suggestion stays as the GM left it: drafting talk again raises no title or Hidden Achievement already suggested to that character, and no card drawn from a line already cited, and the run lists each one held back with why. The catalog's counted titles are not drafted (the bookkeeping drafter ticks their counts). Personal Opportunities are not drafted from talk: the book drafts one at the sweep, from the HVE sheet and the situation (Quests, "Personal Opportunities"), so the drafter for them sits beside the sweep (Gabriel, 2026-09-28: build it there). Alternatives where drafts differ wait until a kind shows two readings worth offering.
- **Claude's calls in drafting Personal Opportunities, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-28):** the GM asks for one character at a time from the HVE section, after the sweep, with the situation in their own words (optional); the request fills the book's template from the record (Grade and level, the Deep leads, the last sweep's Current and its three biggest moments with their events, the circled notes, titles, refusals by flavor, the party, quests held, the last session's summary, the latest five events). A flavor refused 6 times is not offered, and one refused 3 times is marked to be chosen only when it fits far better; a character who has refused all three is refused before any request. The model chooses to affirm or test by the book's rule (Current and Deep leaning different ways, or Salvaged held) and says which. The VE is the Reward Reference Table's, which the record fills in; the model writes the reward hint, a time limit (in hours on the clock when one is set, in words otherwise), and a count marked as one that cannot scale where it cannot. The template's hidden outcome and refusal consequence go in a new GM-only note on the quest, which the holder's log never carries (`questForHolder` strips it). The draft is a suggestion in the panel that opens in the Quests form, filled in, so the GM edits it with every field the form has; issuing it there accepts the draft. The System's words that come with the offer are sent only when the GM sends them from the card, since the offer already brings its own notice. The player-facing text is told never to name the axes, their sides, or the sheet; the table's-words warning covers it on the card. The request states the book's affirm-or-test signal, computed from the record (each axis where the last sweep's Current leads one side and Deep the other, and a held title whose effect says offers arrive as tests), so the model applies the rule without working it out. Offer fixtures (`packages/listening/offers/`, one per branch of the rule) score the stance and the flavor against what the rules decide; the prose is Gabriel's to read, so the eval prints each offer as the player would see it, with the GM's note, and flags what a reader would stop at: a capitalized pole name, the sheet's words, the table's words, a message over three lines, a proportional reward the quest does not pay.
- **Claude's calls in drafting the System's voice, open to Gabriel's cut (2026-09-29):** two drafters share one statement of the voice, built from `rules/system-ai.yaml` (the register, the rules, the units, humor, attention, the names, and one sample per register), so a change to the data reaches both. The composer drafts from what the GM wants said, typed in a box above the message, or from the message already written when the box is empty; the draft replaces the message for the GM to edit and send, so a drafted message and a typed one are the same record. The vision is drafted from the meditation form, from the card, the family chosen, the player's words, and the character's earlier visions (so it does not repeat their images), and fills the vision and the Insight, which the drafter proposes within the book's 1 to 3 with a reason for the GM. Neither is stored or run in the background, since the GM is waiting at the form (messages took 2 to 8 seconds and visions 8 to 15, at medium effort); both controls show only when the campaign has a key. Neither request carries the HVE sheet, and the message drafter is told to state the acts the System measured and never the trait they add up to. The message drafter keeps every number the GM gives, may add a measurement the message needs to be precise, lists each addition for the GM to check, and never adds a reward, a cost, or a deadline. A draft is flagged for what breaks the voice on sight (the table's words, the System speaking of itself, a chatbot's mannerisms, exclamation, brackets or bold, the sheet's words, a behavioral side's capitalized name in a message, the family's or Principle's name in a vision, a vision past 4 sentences), shown until the GM edits it. The book's sample vision joined `rules/system-ai.yaml` (0.1.32) so the prompt reads it from the data.
- **Assigned points proposed from the HVE entries (Gabriel's ruling, 2026-09-29; Claude's calls within it open to his cut):** each row of the Behavioral Stat Mapping is one side of the Hidden Vector Engine, in the axes' order (`rules/character.yaml` `side`, rules 0.1.33; queued for the book), so the GM's Assigned points form proposes a placement from the character's HVE entries since the last placement, with no model call. The window runs from the last placement (or the character's start), so levels landing together share it; each entry counts at its intensity (a reminder at 0.5) and its secondary one weight lower; a class held ends the window. The shape follows the book's reading of the table, with the sweep's own numbers: one side alone at the Defining weight (3) or more puts all three on its primary; a side leading the next by 2 or more (the lead Current needs to move Deep), or alone below 3, puts two on its primary and one on its secondary; three sides level at the top put one on each primary; otherwise the leader's primary takes two and the runner-up's primary one. A tie goes to the side lived most recently. ## Rules editions

Ruled by Gabriel, 2026-09-27. A campaign's log replays under its rules, and the app's code runs only the rules shapes it knows, so it supports a window of versions and retires the rest. Minor versions change numbers and tables; major versions change procedures (`rules/version.yaml`). An edition is a major version, matching a printed book.

| Stage | | Gate | What it holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **active** (built) | none | Every campaign moves to the current rules when the server starts; an action a new version no longer applies lands in the rejected list |
| 2 | *deferred* | the first printed edition (1.0) is locked | Campaigns pin an edition, never a minor version. Minor versions migrate within an edition automatically, and the GM gets a notice of each sheet's changes. A new campaign records its edition |
| 3 | *deferred* | a second edition (2.0) exists | The app runs the current edition and the one before, side by side. Upcasters rewrite an older edition's actions as the log is read. Moving between editions is upcasting plus replay, offered to the GM with a preview, never automatic. When a third edition ships, the oldest edition's campaigns become read-only with an export, unless a migration is written |
| Export | *deferred* | none | A campaign's log as a file: backups, the listening tests, and stage 3's retirement |

## Architecture

- **One TypeScript workspace in `app/`**, managed with pnpm: `packages/engine`, `packages/record`, `packages/server`, and `packages/web` when M1 reaches it. The web client is React with Vite; the server is Node with HTTP and WebSockets and serves the built client too; Postgres holds the record.
- **Hosting (Gabriel, 2026-09-25):** one always-on Fly.io machine in the personal organization, beside a Supabase project (Postgres and Auth) in us-east-2. A single long-running process keeps the live channel, the per-campaign lock, and later the listening streams in one place; a second machine would need its updates passed between machines first. Deploys come from GitHub Actions and wait for an empty table.
- **The engine is pure.** It takes a rules snapshot and computes; it holds no state and does no I/O. The server stores one snapshot per rules version and builds each campaign's engine from the version it pins. The Python engine stays the reference for the book's tables and sims; the fixtures are the contract both engines meet.
- **The record is an append-only action log.** Every change is an action with an idempotency key, an actor (GM, player, accepted suggestion), a source (manual, voice, counter), and what caused it. Sheets are computed from the log. Undo is a compensating action. A correction's preview recomputes the log without the corrected action and shows the difference ("without this award Kara is Level 3, and her Level 4 points return unallocated"); the GM chooses what to apply. Suggestions live apart from the log until accepted.
- **Sign-in is a Supabase account, through Google** (Gabriel, 2026-09-25). The browser sends Supabase's access token and the server checks it against the project's published keys. The actor on every action comes from the token. An invite link makes a signed-in person a player; one GM per campaign.
- **What a player receives is filtered on the server.** A player's view holds their own characters' interfaces (What Can Be Seen, "Your Own Interface") and notices about them, never the log, its length, the Saturation band, or pending assigned points. The GM receives the whole record.
- **AI sits behind adapters** so providers can change without touching the game model: a language model (Claude Opus 5.5 by default) and a speech-to-text service. Deterministic rules never go through a model.

## Voice capture

Each participant opens the app in a browser tab; a player's tab is their character's interface. The tab captures that person's microphone and streams it to the server, and the group talks on Discord as usual. The app carries no conversation between people; it only listens. Each stream is one person, so the server knows who spoke.

Known costs:

- **Headphones.** A laptop speaker lets one microphone hear everyone. When the same words arrive on two streams at once, the server keeps the louder copy.
- **Two mutes.** Muting in Discord does not reach the app. Each tab shows a live capture indicator and its own mute, and a player who steps away to talk in the room mutes the app too.
- **The tab stays open** with microphone permission for the whole session.

A failure in capture loses the listening and leaves the game on Discord. The audio sits behind one interface, so an in-app voice room (LiveKit is the candidate) can replace it later without changes downstream.

## AI context

A model keeps nothing between requests. Every request carries a context the app assembles, and its size stays about constant however long the campaign runs:

1. **Standing context**, always sent: the premise and each character's sheet, Deep reads, and Defining moments (the book's template, `rules/templates/standing-context.txt`).
2. **Rolling summaries.** The GM approves a session summary at wrap-up. The campaign summary is rewritten from the previous one plus the new session's, and each character's chronicle the same way. GM edits are what the next rewrite starts from, so a correction stays corrected.
3. **Retrieval.** The full event log stays in the database; a suggestion that needs a callback gets the few events that match.
4. **The current scene:** the last minutes of transcript and the tracker's state.

Visibility is applied before assembly: a player-facing request never receives GM notes, HVE readings, hidden criteria, or another character's private record.

The order: the script format and the expected-record schema; text evaluation at M2; synthetic and degraded audio at M3; the rehearsal; real sessions kept as fixtures.

### The script format

A script is a YAML file in `packages/listening/scripts/`; the schema is `packages/listening/src/script.ts`.

- **Speakers**: one GM and the players, each player with the characters they hold.
- **Prep**: content packs loaded into Prep before the setup, as the GM loads them (`tutorial`).
- **Setup**: the record before the first line, as the record's own actions (characters, quests held, the spoils).
- **Recorded**: what the table records in the app during the scene (the combat tracker, the dice), each placed after a line, with the d100s the server would roll and the speaker who recorded it. The drafter sees these in the record; proposing one again is a false positive named "already recorded".
- **Lines**: an id, the speaker, the text, and `as` when the line is spoken in character (a character id, or an NPC's name). `t`, seconds from the start, is for real-time runs.
- **Expected actions**: the record's own actions, each citing the lines that evidence it. An event carries its HVE entries in the record's shape; `also` lists other entries that are a right reading (another pole, or the lower of two weights). An `optional` item counts when found and costs nothing when missed.
- **Expected suggestions**: a kind (a Prep cue, a Battle Memory, a title, a Hidden Achievement, a Personal Opportunity, a quest) and a key (the Prep id, the title's name, the quest's code).
- **Quiet lines**: lines that must produce nothing, each with its reason (a rules question, a joke, an inside joke, a hypothetical, a retracted call, NPC speech, a state read aloud, off-topic talk, a player away, crosstalk, planning, and a roll or Clash read out that the app already recorded).

A script is checked by replaying it: the setup, then line by line the expected actions each line starts and the actions recorded after it, through a real campaign record, with the recorded dice. An expected record the rules refuse is a broken script, so a rules change that breaks a scene fails `make app-check`.

The scorer takes a pipeline's drafts, each citing the lines it drew on, and reports precision and recall by category, with the HVE entries scored on their own. A draft matches an expected item when both are the same kind of thing about the same things (the same item moved from and to the same holders, the same quest, the same invitation) and the draft cites one of the item's lines. An event matches on its lines and one character in common; its entries then match by character and pole, and the intensity is reported as exact, accepted through `also`, or off. A drafted event that merges two expected moments matches both, the second on a character it carries an entry for that no earlier match claimed (Gabriel, 2026-09-28), and an entry no matched moment wanted counts as false once. Free text (summaries, intents) is not scored. A draft matching nothing is a false positive, named by the quiet reason when it cites only quiet lines, so a report reads "two hypotheticals logged". The scorer can be limited to the categories a pipeline drafts so far.
