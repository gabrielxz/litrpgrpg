# Gradebreaker — Art Bible & Visual Design Direction

Version 1.0 • 13 September 2026

Scope: illustrations, System graphics, typography, iconography, book layout, website, and companion app. This is the handoff for continuing the project in a fresh conversation or repository. It records the approved direction and separates it from implementation choices still to be tested.

## 1. Start here

**Expressive human imagery, disciplined System geometry, and exceptionally readable information design.**

Gradebreaker brings ordinary contemporary people into contact with an alien reality governed by the System. Its strongest visual material shows recognizable everyday life continuing alongside extraordinary power: survivors meeting around an office table while cyan glyphs hover between them. This relationship is central, not incidental flavor.

The illustration style combines angular cut-paper shape design with matte gouache handling. It is artistically selective: shapes are invented, details are chosen, and areas are intentionally obscured or omitted. The System introduces rigorous luminous geometry into that interpreted physical world.

The user particularly approved the abandoned-office illustration and all three restrained color variations. These are the principal visual standards. The terminal, meditation, monk, battle, and bone cover demonstrate range. Do not restart style exploration unless requested.

### Using this kit in a new project

1. Add this document as project context.
2. Use `Project-Instructions.md` as the concise behavioral brief for future art work.
3. Unpack the kit and make the relevant PNG references available as images. Do not assume a filename or Markdown link alone gives an image generator visual access.
4. Start with `references/01-office-bone.png` and one approved color variant. For another subject, explicitly label these as **style references only**.
5. Give the new scene, intended placement, aspect ratio, and System intent. Avoid importing the reference's characters or composition unintentionally.

The written specification is useful but has not reproduced the full look reliably without visual references. Reference-guided generation is the preferred workflow. No generator or future session is guaranteed to match automatically.

## 3. Illustration construction

### Shape before texture

Compose with large light and dark masses. Build bodies, architecture, and objects from irregular interlocking planes. Use crisp directional changes against selected softer edges. Some contours dissolve into neighboring shadows; others assert themselves sharply. The picture should work as an arrangement of shapes before it is inspected for detail.

Texture supports this construction. It is not the mechanism that makes a realistic rendering artistic. Local pigment grain, rough cut edges, and occasional brush marks are welcome. The approved references contain mottling; preserve their tactile character without increasing it or making every surface look like chipped stone. Completely flat passages provide relief. Do not overcorrect into sterile vector clip art or a uniformly low-poly render.

Priority order:

1. Composition and expressive shape design.
2. Readable activity and recognizable setting.
3. Selective description and quiet areas.
4. Purposeful System phenomena.
5. Surface texture and small detail.

### People

Figures must be designed at the silhouette level, not naturally rounded bodies filled with angular patches. Use broken straight segments, asymmetric shoulders, sharply bent elbows, tapered forearms, wedge-like cuffs, irregular hems, and angular hair masses. Curves remain available for gesture and humanity; avoid uniform jaggedness.

Faces use decisive cheek, jaw, brow, and nose planes with a few expressive marks. Garments use a handful of large folds and shadow wedges that reshape the silhouette. Allow slight compression, elongation, or distortion when it improves posture or composition. Hands must communicate the action without anatomical microdetail.

Preserve varied ages, bodies, skin tones, and personalities. Do not drift into identical young male protagonists, heroic physiques, or a homogeneous crowd. Contemporary clothes should remain recognizable where the scene calls for them. Alien bracers, weapons, crystals, and armor supplement those clothes rather than automatically replacing them.

Avoid soft cartoon outlining, realistic faces beneath a paint filter, intricate wrinkles, polished CGI, and mechanical polygonization. The second laundromat test gained angularity but still did not match the approved office's broader visual character. Angularity alone is insufficient.

### Settings and detail

Backgrounds may be present and spatially complete. Simplify them through broad architectural or landscape shapes and selected identifying features. Depth comes from overlap, scale, value grouping, and composition. Expressive perspective is allowed, but objects and actions should remain intelligible.

Quiet areas can be a pale wall, sky, window, shadow, or broad color plane. Empty black is an option, not a requirement. Do not impose a fixed percentage of blank space on every image.

Abandonment needs a telling omission or broken fixture, not a carpet of trash. A city can be a few layered silhouettes. A battle can imply distant ranks through grouped marks while resolving only the important foreground interactions. Detail should explain the story, not demonstrate how much can be rendered.

### Range across a book

Vary close-ups and wide scenes, light-dominant and dark-dominant compositions, quiet and energetic moments, environmental context and isolated figures. Include repair, teaching, conversation, rest, and practical activity alongside battles and advancement. Do not make every illustration a spectacular portal scene.

## 4. Color and illumination

### Stable foundation, flexible scene palette

Warm bone ivory, chalk, warm gray, and deep blue-black anchor the family. They are a foundation, not a prohibition on color. Luminous cyan is the recurring System signature.

Approved palette modes:

| Mode | Application |
| --- | --- |
| Bone foundation | Near-neutral physical world; cyan owns the saturated focal area |
| Everyday color | Small local muted colors attached to belongings: faded brick hoodie, olive chair, ochre mug |
| Coordinated scene palette | A few larger related color planes, such as sage walls and clay garments |
| Time-of-day color | Broad warm apricot daylight and lavender-gray shadow planes, while preserving the graphic drawing |

All modes may coexist in the book. Vary the amount and distribution of color. Natural skin tones are welcome in simplified planes. Physical colors are generally matte and restrained; System light is conspicuously emissive and precise. Do not distribute accents evenly across every surface.

Gold, wine, lavender, olive, and ochre were explored as scene accents. None is a mandatory secondary brand color or an established lore category. Gold can suggest physical brass or an older artifact; this is an available interpretation, not a fixed meaning.

Ordinary light is expressed primarily through broad value and color shapes. Controlled glow is allowed for the System, including selective reflections on bodies, equipment, and architecture. The original blanket ban on smooth gradients is superseded: reject photographic modeling, not every soft luminous edge.

Do not create overlapping color codes for chapters, factions, energy types, and statuses before their needs are established. Keep digital status colors available, with labels and symbols so color is never the sole carrier of meaning.

Exact hex values and print conversions remain to be chosen and tested. The approved images are visual anchors, not a claim that one sampled pixel is the canonical color.

## 5. System visual grammar

The viewer should increasingly recognize what the System is doing without translating its script. Build a grammar of intent, not a decorative alphabet pasted onto every object.

### Three layers

- **Glyphs carry information:** varied angular characters arranged into strings or clusters.
- **Geometry expresses the operation:** enclosing, connecting, dividing, measuring, aligning.
- **Light and placement indicate activity:** dormant, addressing a subject, executing, or failing.

### Character construction

Use open triangles, stepped strokes, incomplete nested squares, forks, diagonal bars, and occasional detached dots. Maintain consistent construction principles while varying individual forms. Prefer deliberate short strings and coherent arrangements over random neon squiggles or repeated logos. Characters must be unreadable and must not contain accidental Latin text, numbers, or conventional HUD microtext.

The earlier user-supplied glyph references demonstrate script bands, radial diagrams, inscriptions, projections onto faces/equipment, and ruptures through physical surfaces. Borrow their glyph vocabulary and integration, not their older dense ink, parchment, or hatching style.

### Intent mapping

| Intent | Geometry and arrangement | Weight and light |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation / evaluation | Open brackets, fine radial ticks, sparse glyphs oriented toward a subject | Fine, precise, localized |
| Communication / instruction | Ordered strings, generous spacing, a clear directional arrangement | Legible as shapes, measured emphasis |
| Recognition / advancement | Separate fragments resolving into alignment or near-completion | A few assertive marks; concentrated illumination |
| Restriction / judgment | Closed boundaries, repeated bars, tight spacing | Heavier strokes, firm continuous structures |
| Instability / overload | Interrupted continuity, displaced layers, competing orientations | Uneven activation or overprinted components |
| Dormant / residual | Partial inscriptions, incomplete traces | Low contrast, little or no emission |

This mapping is an adopted art-direction convention, not yet a tested set of final symbol assets.

**Boldness expresses force; density expresses complexity.** A powerful command can be one huge mark. A complex analysis can consist of many fine marks. Importance does not always mean more material.

Even under instability, keep individual components exact. Disrupt their alignment, repetition, continuity, and relationships so the same underlying intelligence remains visible.

### Placement and restraint

System graphics may float independently, inhabit a surface, project across a subject, frame an object, or organize an energy flow. Choose placement for the scene's meaning. Radial diagrams are approved and effective, but not compulsory. Avoid giving every scene the same halo or central diamond.

Subtle effects still need intentional visible glyph shapes; subtle must not collapse into illegible microtext. Reserve dense detail for a focal operation with at most a few supporting echoes. Use clear scene-specific logic rather than arbitrary glitter.

## 6. Functional icons and recurring graphics

Functional icons must have stable meanings; System glyphs may have expressive variation. Keep these systems related but separate.

Create a small coherent icon family for recurring concepts such as action, time, energy, equipment, and conditions, based on the final rules. Use consistent stroke weights, angles, scale, and negative space. Test at actual small sizes. Pair unfamiliar symbols with labels. Reserve circles and radial forms mainly for processes, quantities, and cycles rather than making every icon a magical clock.

Final icon drawings and mappings have not been created. Do not treat generated glyph art as a production icon library.

### The interrupted frame

Use a boundary that remains understandable despite missing sections as a recurring device. It relates to incomplete System diagrams, partially described architecture, and selective drawing. It can appear in chapter openings, callouts, image crops, or selected UI states. A small cyan segment may complete part of the boundary.

Use this sparingly. Do not put a broken box around every paragraph or clip every illustration into the same shape. Exact line weights and proportions are still to be designed.

## 7. Typography and lettering

Two typographic voices are intended:

| Role | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Human-facing book prose | Sturdy readable serif with enough substance to sit beside angular art; avoid faux-medieval ornament |
| System messages, labels, statistics | Restrained composed sans serif; avoid stereotypical sci-fi cutouts and missing strokes |
| Book title and possibly chapter titles | A dedicated display treatment, potentially with one controlled interruption or displaced segment |
| Dense app controls and data | Clear readable type selected for scanning; semantic roles take priority over copying prose typography everywhere |

Exact families are not selected. Typeface selection must be tested with actual prose, rules, numerals, tables, emphasis, and System messages. Check licensing for print, embedding, and web use before committing. Explore tabular numerals for aligned statistics where supported. Do not introduce a third decorative face merely to make the design feel more thematic.

Ordinary headings remain intact. System messages are actual readable text, with glyphs alongside or framing them. The prohibition on readable words applies to generated illustration content, not to the book or application.

Compose final titles, rules, labels, and tables in layout software or the app's text system, not as text baked into generated illustrations. No final font files, wordmark, or typography specimen has been approved yet.

## 8. Book layout

Default reading pages should be warm ivory with dark text, generous margins, and restrained cyan details. Let the book have quiet pages as well as quiet regions within images. Save expansive blue-black backgrounds for selected chapter openings and major System passages.

| Content | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Rules | Stable alignment and hierarchy; little decoration |
| Examples of play | Modest tint or a consistent simple margin marker |
| In-world System message | Readable sans text, precise frame fragments, selective glyph notation |
| Fiction / atmosphere | More open composition; art may interrupt a page edge |
| Tables | Clear spacing and column hierarchy; minimal ornamental framing |

Keep the human world recognizable and usable. Every sidebar need not resemble an alien artifact. Preserve separation between descriptive fiction and actual rules. Never obscure essential instructions with background artwork.

Mix full scenes, portraits, isolated objects, and small spot illustrations. Design each for its placement. Crops must protect faces, meaningful hands, and focal glyphs; do not place essential action across a gutter. A cover needs space for title and other typography, but not every interior image needs a blank top quarter.

Trim size, grid, margins, type sizes, line spacing, column count, bleed, and running furniture remain open. The next layout test should include prose, a rules table, an example, a System message, and one approved illustration in a representative spread. A generated picture of a spread is not proof of typographic usability.

## 9. Website and companion app

The digital interface can feel like the orderly System through which the reader interacts with the book's rougher world. Carry the visual principles across while allowing functional adaptation.

| Carry across | Digital expression |
| --- | --- |
| Bone / blue-black / cyan | Clean light and dark surfaces with consistent emphasis |
| Angular forms | Restrained corners, dividers, and shape relationships |
| Interrupted arcs | Selected process indicators and meaningful state graphics |
| Cut-paper illustration | Headers, portraits, onboarding, feature moments, empty states where useful |
| Glyph language | Decorative identity alongside understandable controls and labels |
| System energy | Brief, purposeful effects for advancement, refinement, or other meaningful events |

Keep character sheets, tables, forms, and frequent controls quiet. Avoid textured panels everywhere, glowing buttons everywhere, rotating glyphs behind text, or elaborate HUD frames around every statistic. Functional icons are stable; illustrative glyphs vary.

Reserve an unambiguous treatment for interactive elements so decorative cyan does not make everything appear clickable. Support keyboard focus, readable contrast, text resizing, and reduced motion. Pair status colors with words or icons. These are implementation requirements to test, not a claim that any interface has already passed accessibility checks.

Compose illustrations for responsive crops and small screens. Use vector assets for precise UI frames, icons, and repeatable System geometry; use raster assets for paintings. Do not export an entire functional screen as one image. Optimize web derivatives while retaining original masters.

No app mockup, component library, or complete website has been produced as part of this direction.

### Approaches that were superseded

- Dense stencil/sumi-e splatter, distressed parchment, pervasive cross-hatching, rubble-heavy scenes.
- Cinematic realistic figures or environments, even under artistic color grading.
- Treating every object as an obligation to render.
- Completely blank backgrounds as a mandatory rule.
- A single accent color forced across all scenes.
- Identical giant glyph emblems in every picture.

The text-only laundromat experiments are not canonical. The first had soft naturalistic contours and conventional HUD microtext. The second added angularity but became more mechanically polygonal and still missed the approved art's broader composition, tonal handling, and tactile character. Do not “fix” the art bible by adding ever more angularity. The approved images remain necessary calibration.

## 11. Generation workflow and reusable brief

### Practical workflow

1. Choose the scene and its emotional purpose; specify what the people are doing.
2. Choose placement and aspect ratio before composing.
3. Supply one or two relevant approved style references. Add a glyph reference only when its construction is needed, with its limited role explicit.
4. Choose palette mode and System intent. Do not automatically use every element of the grammar.
5. Generate, compare with the office standard, and revise one or two concrete discrepancies.
6. Save approved outputs as new references with a note explaining what was approved. Never let an unapproved experiment silently redefine the style.

For edits, identify the exact target and invariants. For new scenes, explicitly forbid copying reference characters or composition unless requested. A later explicit user choice may supersede this bible; record the change rather than inventing a new standing rule.

### Reusable generation instruction

> Create an original Gradebreaker illustration using the supplied approved office image as a style reference only. Preserve its angular cut-paper/gouache construction, selective tactile texture, strong light-dark masses, expressive human figures, and deliberately simplified but recognizable surroundings. Match its artistic character rather than copying its scene.
>
> Build people through angular silhouettes and large expressive clothing planes. Keep them human and individually posed. Do not make them photorealistic, softly cartooned, mechanically low-poly, or uniformly jagged. Avoid adding detail merely because an object is present.
>
> Anchor the physical world in bone ivory, gray, and blue-black, with the selected restrained scene colors. Cyan System effects should use precise invented glyph strings and purposeful geometry, with weight, density, and placement suited to the specified intent. Keep ordinary light and System emission distinct. Do not render readable text, numbers, logos, or conventional HUD microtext inside the illustration.
>
> Use large quiet areas and meaningful background features. Let texture support shapes; do not coat the image in a filter. Follow the scene and placement brief below.

Append:

| Field | Supply |
| --- | --- |
| Scene | People, activity, location, emotional purpose |
| Placement | Cover / spread / half-page / portrait / spot / web header |
| Format | Aspect ratio and requested pixel target if supported |
| Palette | Bone / everyday color / coordinated scene / time-of-day |
| System | Intent, integration with subject, visual prominence |
| Must show | A few identifying story details |
| May omit | Background or object details that need not be resolved |
| Reference roles | Style anchor, glyph vocabulary, edit target if applicable |

## 12. Resolution, print, and production

The packaged landscape references are 1536 × 1024 pixels; the portrait references are 1024 × 1536. They establish art direction, not final full-page print readiness.

Print dimensions are determined by pixels divided by intended pixels per inch. At a planning value of 300 ppi, 1536 × 1024 covers approximately 5.12 × 3.41 inches. An 8 × 10 inch image area would require 2400 × 3000 pixels at that density, before any added bleed. These are arithmetic examples, not a chosen trim size or a printer specification.

Larger image generation is available through some OpenAI image workflows. Official API documentation describes 4K-class dimensions, with model-specific restrictions. That does not establish exact sizing controls in every ChatGPT session. The built-in tool used during this exploration exposes a prompt and references, not a dedicated exact-size parameter. Request larger output when needed, then inspect the actual file; do not promise that a prompt guarantees dimensions. Source checked 13 September 2026: [OpenAI image-generation documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation).

Before final production, set intended physical size, bleed, and printer requirements; generate the appropriate master; inspect at placement size. Upscaling can enlarge a file but does not create authentic original detail or automatically make it press-ready. Increasing a DPI metadata tag does not increase pixels.

Keep art separate from typography, logos, and exact functional diagrams. Preserve RGB masters and prepare print conversions for the selected printer. Cyan that appears luminous on a screen must be conveyed in print by contrast; ordinary printing does not emit light. Proof cyan, dark shadow separation, and warm bone tones. Avoid crushing entire figures into black.

## 13. Review criteria

- Does it belong beside the office reference without relying solely on the palette?
- Are people artistically constructed, individually posed, and readable?
- Does the setting communicate place without an inventory of detail?
- Does the mundane/magical relationship serve the scene?
- Are quiet areas purposeful and the action clear at intended size?
- Does the System imply an operation or mood, with varied meaningful glyph construction?
- Are accidental readable characters, generic HUD microtext, anatomical failures, and nonsensical interactions absent?
- Does texture support rather than replace shape design?
- Across several pages, do composition, palette, scale, and glyph placement vary?
- For layouts and UI, can information be read and operated without decoding decoration?

## 14. Next decisions, when needed

Continue reference-guided image production without another lengthy portability experiment. Separately, make a representative book spread to choose the actual type pairing and page structure. Then develop the interrupted-frame treatment and a small functional icon set. Test a real companion-app screen with dense information before expanding the UI vocabulary. Choose final color values and print settings in context rather than by swatch alone.

Version changes should record the decision, its reason, and the newly approved evidence. Preserve the current office references as the baseline until explicitly superseded.
