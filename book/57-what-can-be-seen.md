# What Can Be Seen

The System shows every Integrated being a great deal about themselves, rather less about each other, and nothing at all about what it has concluded. This chapter collects the whole visibility question in one place: what a character reads off their own interface, what they can learn by looking at somebody else, what a party shares, and what stays hidden from everyone including the person it describes.

The systems being made visible are owned elsewhere. Titles and their categories are in the Titles chapter, the quest log is in System Quests, and the behavioral record is in The Hidden Vector Engine. What follows is only who gets to look.

---

## Your Own Interface

Every Integrated being can summon a **System interface**: a private overlay, called up by intent, showing what the System is willing to tell a person about themselves. It costs nothing and takes no Beat. Reading it carefully in the middle of a fight is a different matter, and a GM is right to ask for a Beat when a player wants to study something rather than glance at it.

Nobody else can see your interface. It is not projected, it cannot be shown to a friend, and there is no way to hand it over. What another character can learn about you is covered further down, and it is much less than this.

**The interface displays:**

- Every Attribute, in both readings: the Raw value and the Force the table actually rolls.
- Health, Aether, and stored Volatile Energy against Tolerance.
- Level, Grade, and progress toward the next level.
- Proficiencies, their tiers, and the Marks accumulated in each.
- The Principle: its family while resonance is still accruing, its name once crystallized, its tier, and its Insight.
- Every title held, including the ones being kept hidden from other people.
- The quest log, active, completed, and failed.
- Unallocated stat points, waiting until they are spent.

**The interface does not display:**

- Anything the Hidden Vector Engine holds. Not the axes, not the values, not the fact that a particular moment was logged.
- The criteria for any title, quest, or achievement that has not yet fired.
- Anything at all about another person.

**This is what the character sheet is.** The paper in front of a player is the same object their character is looking at, which is why the two carry the same information in the same order, and why the behavioral record is on the GM's side of the screen instead. The System has never shown anyone their own vector.

::: systemvoice
*[Interface: available. Behavioral telemetry: not available.]*

*[This message will not be repeated.]*
:::

**GM guidance.** Players will ask what the interface says about something the System has not decided to tell them. The answer is always the same shape: the interface reports state and never intention. It will show a character that they hold 40 Insight in Weight. It will not tell them what happens at 50, whether their behavior is drifting, or what the System thinks they are becoming.

## What You See of Others

Looking at another Integrated being tells you something, and how much depends almost entirely on the gap between you.

| **Inspector's Grade against the target** | **What they see** |
|---|---|
| Lower Grade | Nothing. A higher-Grade being does not resolve. |
| Same Grade | Achievement and HVE-Resonant titles. |
| One Grade higher | The above, plus *worn* Bestowed titles. |
| Two Grades higher | The above, plus *hidden* Bestowed titles. |
| Three or more Grades higher | Everything, including Hidden Achievement titles. |

Nobody at any Grade sees another person's Attributes, Health, Aether, Insight, or quest log. Titles are the only thing the System publishes, which is why they function as reputation: a title is the one part of a character that other people can read.

**Being unable to inspect is information.** A character who looks at something and gets nothing back has learned that they are outclassed, and that is often the most useful sentence a GM can deliver. Say it plainly: *you look, and it does not resolve.*

**Worn and hidden.** Bestowed titles can be worn or hidden at the holder's discretion, and switching costs nothing but a moment's attention. A worn title is legible to every observer of equal or higher Grade. A hidden one is concealed from peers and transparent to anyone two Grades up, which means concealment works on the people you meet and fails against the people you should be worried about.

**Hidden Achievement titles are private** until the holder chooses otherwise. Revealing one is deliberate and permanent; after that it reads like a worn Bestowed title. This is the mechanism players use to brag, and they should be allowed to enjoy it.

**Negative titles cannot be hidden.** The System publishes its judgments. "Oathbroken" and "Salvaged" are worn whether their holder likes it or not, and everyone of equal or higher Grade can see them.

## What a Party Shares

Formal party membership opens one narrow window that inspection never provides: the **party frame**, showing each member's current Health, maximum Health, Aether, and whether they are Downed. Nothing else. Party membership does not expose Attributes, titles beyond ordinary inspection, Insight, or anyone's log.

That narrow window is worth more than it looks. Knowing an ally's true condition changes every triage decision at the table, and it is the reason strangers in this genre form a party before they trust each other: the frame is a fact where a person's word is not. The full rules for forming a party are in System Quests.

## What Nobody Sees

**The Hidden Vector Engine is invisible to everyone.** Not to other players, not to inspection at any Grade differential, and not to the character it describes. There is no in-fiction instrument that reads it and no title that reveals it. A character can infer their own pattern the way anyone infers their own character, by noticing what they keep doing, and that inference is exactly as reliable as it is in life.

**Hidden Quests do not appear in the log** until they resolve, and sometimes never resolve at all. **Hidden Achievement criteria are never published in advance.** The System does not warn anyone that they are three sealed doors into something.

**The System does not explain itself.** It reports, it grants, and it withholds, and none of the withholding is negotiable. A character who demands to know why gets the same non-response every time.

**This is a rule about the table as much as the fiction.** A player who can read their own dials starts steering them, and the moment behavior becomes performance the Engine is measuring the performance instead of the person. The machinery is public and the readings are private, which is the reading stance the Introduction sets out for the whole book.

## Open Design Space

Deferred rather than decided:

- **Inspection as a contested action.** Everything above resolves off Grade alone. Whether a character can invest in reading people better, and whether another can invest in being harder to read, is unbuilt. It is the natural home for a PER-based skill and for the first equipment that hides a title from a peer.
- **False readings.** Nothing currently lets a character present a title they do not hold, or a Grade they have not reached. The genre is full of exactly that, and it belongs with the deception Proficiency when it is built.
- **What higher-Grade beings volunteer.** The table above says what an observer can take. What a powerful being chooses to show on purpose, and what it costs them to suppress, is Aura Pressure's territory and is only half-specified.
