## Halden, the worked region

**[proposal]** A river city of about two hundred thousand, on both banks of the Halden, joined by one road bridge and one rail bridge. The gate opened on Day 2 in the arch of the old courthouse on Court Square, on the north bank. The diagram stands over the square. Neighborhoods a GM can point at:

- **The Flats**, south bank: warehouses, the transit depot, the co-op market, the working river. Prevailing stance: **use**.
- **Northgate**, north bank around Court Square: the hospital, the concert hall, the radio mast on the hill. Prevailing stance: **worship**, centered on the hall's shelter.
- **The Bend**, upriver on the north bank: a residential grid where the street councils formed on Day 1. Prevailing stance: **refuse**.

One institution that works: the hospital, short-staffed and open. One that is failing: the water utility, whose plant is in the Flats and whose engineers were half taken. The gate changed the routes: the road bridge is a Rift (band two), so the two banks are joined by the rail bridge on foot and by Namar's barges once the river is open. Court Square is the customs table, the market, and the shrine at once, and that is the dispute.

**The three communities, each divided:**

| Community | Spokesperson | Dissenting voice | The dispute |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Bend (refuse) | **Ana Kowalczyk**, who chaired the first street meeting and keeps the roof crews working | **Mike Tran**, Level 3 by Day 3, who cleared the gym alone and wants the council to admit it helped | Whether a cultivator can live on the street. The council's compromise: he can sleep there and cannot recruit there. |
| Northgate (worship) | **Pastor Lin Okonkwo**, whose title was granted in the hall in front of two hundred people | **Dr. Sefa Aydin**, who runs the hospital and wants the hall's people working instead of waiting for notices | Whether the depot stays as it is. The congregation holds its evening vigil at the depot fence, where the title was granted, and the Bearer's circuit has become part of the service. |

**Selan ar Nerava** **[proposal]**: second form, a freight dispatcher for the port on the far side of the gate. ## The Fault Line: three responses and three posts

**Refuse.** The community will not level, will not take quests, and treats cultivators as collaborators or as weapons it did not ask for. What it looks like: a neighborhood that took the diagram off its phones, a council that voted. What it wants: cultivators gone, or silent, or useful without a notice. What it fears: the day the System asks its children something. What moves it: a Rift cleared at the hospital it uses; a cultivator who takes no payment and no notice for it; a cultivator who takes a Mandate against its wishes.

**Worship.** The community has read the message as a calling. What it looks like: a shelter run by the two highest-level people in it, a sign on a door listing who has a title, a vigil where a title was granted. What it wants: what the System wants, as it understands that; cultivators to lead. What it fears: being passed over. What moves it: a Personal Opportunity refused in public; a title granted in front of it; a cultivator who explains what the System actually recorded.

**Use.** The community treats the System as weather and cultivators as a resource. What it looks like: a customs desk at the gate, a co-op paying in food for a Rift cleared, a mayor with a list. What it wants: Rifts cleared, routes open, terms. What it fears: dependence on people it cannot govern. What moves it: a bargain kept; a bargain broken; a cultivator who does the work without terms, which frightens it more than a price.

A community's response moves when the party does something it can see. Say what it saw and let the response follow. The stance is written in words on the region sheet and changes when the GM says it has.

**Still Here, refuse:**

> "We had a meeting. Forty-one people, the whole street. Nobody's taking the quests. Nobody's touching the glowing ones. If it wants to watch us, it can watch us fix the roof. Mike says that's stupid and he's already Level 3 and I said then you sleep at Mike's. I don't care what it wrote down about me. I care what my daughter thinks I did."

**Still Here, worship:**

> "Day 5. Pastor Lin got a title in front of everyone, right there in the hall, and you could see it. I don't know how to explain it to people who weren't there. Some of you are calling it a prison. It told us the rules. Nobody ever told us the rules before. My whole life nobody told me the rules."

**Still Here, use:**

> "OK practical people only. Marguerite at the co-op will feed a team of four for a week per cleared site, she's got a list, she's not negotiating, she's got the food. Bring your own bandages. Do NOT go near the depot on Fourth, there's one there that goes for anything that steps over the paint and it does not stop for a while."

(The first is confident and partial: the neighbor at Level 3 is the point. The second is exactly right about one thing. The third is wrong by scope: the depot's Bearer stops when the yard goes silent, and the seed says so.)

## Accession Rifts: the seed catalog

Every Imprint is four lines: what it detects, what triggers it, what it does, what stops it. Its behavior is discoverable and reproducible; a defeated Imprint stays defeated; consequences persist. A Rift is written as **shape, site, objective, boundary, cue, the four lines, completion, reward, what leaving it costs.** A Rift pays the Faction column at its difficulty (25 / 50 / 90 / 150 at Moderate / Hard / Severe / Peak) and states the reward in the notice.

Force follows the sizing table: band one uses the tier rows (Force 25 is a Moderate-to-Hard creature for Levels 4 to 9); from Level 8, the Force rows (standard 40 / 50 / 60 / 70).

**Reading the lines at the table.** *Detects* is the sense the Imprint has and nothing else: a Mast Imprint that detects the carrier hum is blind to a person standing still beside it. *Triggers* is the condition; anything that does not meet it is ignored, and a decoy that meets it is the oldest trick. *Does* is a committed sequence with a length in rounds; the Imprint strikes nothing outside it. *Stops* is the reset. A player who has watched one full cycle can state all four lines, and the GM confirms them.

### Band one, Levels 4 to 9: the first wave

Archived patterns of dead worlds, dropped in the first hour. All six run the **Interceptor** block (Force 25).

1. **The Pumping Station (Hold).** The water plant in the Flats. Objective: keep the pumps running for six hours; the panel needs a hand on it once an hour. Boundary: the yellow line around the plant floor. Cue: the first person to cross it. Detects: movement on the plant floor. Triggers: anything crossing the line. Does: rushes to the crossing point, strikes the first thing there, continues to the far wall, and walks back to its post; the whole movement is two rounds, and it strikes nothing else during it. Stops: when nothing has crossed for a minute; it stands at its post. Completion: six hours of pressure. Reward: Moderate. Leaving it: the Flats drink from the river within two days.
2. **The Overpass (Passage).** The one dry road under the rail bridge between the Bend and Northgate's hospital. Objective: forty people across. Boundary: the median line, and a second Interceptor at the far ramp with a line of its own. Cue: the first crossing. Each Interceptor: detects its own ramp; triggers on anything across its own line; does the two-round rush, strike, run to the barrier, and return; stops when its line has been quiet for a minute. Two lines, two two-round cycles; a crossing runs during the return legs, which a decoy at each line can set off in step. Completion: forty across, or both Interceptors down. Reward: Moderate. Leaving it: the Bend is cut off from the hospital.
3. **The Pharmacy (Claim).** The hospital's pharmacy corridor. Objective: take the corridor and hold it for one day. Boundary: the double doors. Cue: the doors opening. Detects: the corridor. Triggers: anything in the corridor while the doors stand open. Does: advances to the far end over two rounds, striking whatever it reaches, then holds the far end. Stops: when the corridor has been empty for a minute; it walks back to the near end. Completion: the corridor held from dawn to dawn while the pharmacy is emptied. Reward: Hard. Leaving it: the hospital rations, and the forum names who ran out.
4. **The Mast (Hold).** The radio mast on Northgate hill, the region's forum relay. Objective: keep the transmitter on through a night. Boundary: the fence. Cue: the carrier hum. Detects: the hum, and nothing else. Triggers: the transmitter on. Does: walks to the transmitter and strikes it, or whatever stands between, one strike a round. Stops: when the hum stops; it stands still until the hum returns. The objective is the trigger. Completion: six hours on air. Reward: Moderate. Leaving it: Halden goes dark on the forum.
5. **The Gym (Passage).** A school gym in the Bend with two hundred people sheltering in it. Objective: everyone out. Boundary: the doorway. Cue: the first person to leave. Detects: the doorway. Triggers: anything leaving; never anything entering. Does: strikes the leaver and follows three steps, one round, then returns to the door. Stops: when no one has left for a minute. Completion: the gym empty. Reward: Hard. Leaving it: two hundred people fed through the windows.
6. **The Far Side (Passage, on the Aru side).** Selan's four dispatch workers, stranded since Day 2 in the freight yard across the gate. Boundary: the yard gate. Cue: a crossing. Detects: the three-legged gait. Triggers: an Aru crossing the yard. Does: the two-round rush and strike. Stops: when the yard is quiet for a minute. A human walks in unnoticed; the pattern was recorded on a world that never held an Aru's shape. The party can bring four people out that no Aru can reach, and Selan can reach nothing on Earth's side without a human, which is what neither side can do alone. Completion: four workers across the gate. Reward: Hard, and standing with the port. Leaving it: Selan's port has no reason to deal with Halden. The System records the cooperation; a paired Opportunity follows when it has seen the two act together again.
   *Option:* **The Gate (Passage, two-sided)**, for a region whose stayers never cleared their gate: one Interceptor each side, each visible only from its own side, each with the two-round cycle; a crossing timed to both return legs, counted aloud across the plane. The first-contact deed at the party's own gate. Reward: Hard. Leaving it: the gate stays a wall.

### Band two, Levels 10 to 15: the second wave

The **Bearer** block (Force 45): it walks a route carrying a thing and strikes what stands on the route.

7. **The Bridge (Passage).** The road bridge, and a Bearer walking a sealed System cache across it and back on the hour. Objective: a convoy across, or the river opened for the barges below (the Bearer's circuit crosses the channel). Boundary: the bridge deck. Cue: the Bearer's hour. Detects: the deck. Triggers: anything on the deck when it passes. Does: strikes once and walks on; it never stops for a target. Stops: at the far bank, where it turns. Completion: the convoy across, or the Bearer down and the cache open. Reward: Hard; the cache holds a Standard Attribute Treasure. Leaving it: the banks stay a rail bridge apart and the barges stay downstream.
8. **The Workshop (Claim).** The machine shop behind the depot that Sava (the Kith mechanic) and a human repairer both want, and the Maker's recurring scene. Objective: take it and keep it a day. Boundary: the shop floor. Cue: a machine running. Detects: running machines. Triggers: a machine switched on. Does: walks to it and strikes the machine until it stops, then holds beside it. Stops: when the floor is silent for a minute; it returns to the door. Completion: the shop held and one machine run through a shift. Reward: Hard, and the shop, which is the reward the party wanted; every tool in it counts as materials on hand (Classes, "Maker"). Leaving it: the region repairs nothing it cannot repair by hand.
9. **The Depot (Claim).** The transit depot, forty buses, and the vigil at its fence. Objective: take it and keep it a day. Boundary: the depot gates. Cue: an engine starting. Detects: engines. Triggers: a running engine inside the gates. Does: walks to it and strikes the vehicle until it stops. Stops: when the yard is silent. Completion: the depot held and a bus driven out. Reward: Hard. Leaving it: Halden walks. Northgate's congregation wants the depot as it is, because the title was granted at that fence and the Bearer's evening circuit is part of the vigil; the Flats want the buses; Teodor will not send a driver in. Clearing it costs standing with one and earns it with the other, and a party that clears it during the vigil earns neither.

### Band three, Levels 16 to 20: the third wave

The **Cordon** block (Force 60, Yields): it holds a perimeter and gives ground on it rather than off it.

10. **The Hall (Hold).** The concert hall, Northgate's shelter for three weeks and empty now, and a Cordon on the concourse ring. Objective: a night of music, the first since Day 1, with the hall full and Aru in the seats. Boundary: the concourse. Cue: the house lights. Detects: the ring. Triggers: anything on the ring while the house lights are up. Does: advances around the ring one Zone a round striking what it meets, and Yields two Beats to stay on the ring rather than be driven off it. Stops: when the ring is empty for a minute. Completion: the performance finished. Reward: Severe, and the performance, which pays nothing and is the point. Leaving it: the hall stays a shelter with nobody in it.
11. **The Square (Passage).** Court Square, the market Halden and the port both want and the customs table the Flats do not trust. Objective: a market day, with goods crossing the gate both ways. Boundary: the square. Cue: the first stall. Detects: the square's paving. Triggers: anything standing still on it for longer than a round. Does: advances to it and strikes; a target that keeps moving is never struck. Stops: when nothing has stood still for a minute. A market is people standing still. Completion: a day's trade. Reward: Severe, and standing with the port and the Flats both. Leaving it: trade goes through the warehouse yard on Selan's terms.
12. **The Courthouse (Claim).** The gate's own building, and the Bend's meeting place since Day 4: the council meets in its basement because the Cordon on the steps keeps every cultivator in Halden out, and it is the one room in the city where nobody comes to recruit. Objective: take the building and keep it a day. Boundary: the steps. Cue: the doors. Detects: the steps. Triggers: anything on them. Does: holds the top step and strikes what climbs; Yields to hold the step. Stops: when the steps are empty for a minute. Completion: the building held. Reward: Severe. Leaving it: the Bend keeps its wall, and says so. The seed where clearing a Rift costs standing with people the party may like.

### Band four, Levels 21 to 25: the Completion

13. **The Completion (Claim).** The diagram takes a body on Court Square (the sequence is in The Completion, below). The **Completion** block (Force 75, three Beats, Yields two of three), its four lines written from the region's record. Objective: take the square and keep it. Boundary: the diagram's footprint, the largest Rift in Halden. Completion: the Completion down. Reward: Peak, and the site exposed. ## The Completion: one sequence

1. **Warning signs (band three).** The diagram moves: a section completes at a time, and each completion drops a third-wave Imprint (the Cordons). The forum notices before the party does. The GM narrates a completed section after each band-three Rift, so the party can count what is left.
2. **Manifestation (default when the party reaches Level 20; the GM moves it).** The last section completes and the diagram takes a body on the square. The region's largest Rift opens around it. What it draws is the region: the conduct the System recorded in Halden during accession, the party's prominent among it because their actions mattered, and the region's beside it: the evacuation of the hall, the vigil, the street councils, the co-op's list.
3. **Confrontation (band four).** The Completion's block is fixed; its four lines come from the record. **The procedure:** take the region's three most-repeated recorded conducts (the party's Deep Vector from the Session-End Sweeps, and two from the communities) and write them as detects / triggers / does / stops. **The ready default:** detects the party's lead attacker's approach; triggers on the first attack against it; does what the party does in its first round (the GM names the party's habitual opening); stops when everyone in its Rift is standing still, which is the Square's lesson turned around. **Two worked examples** in the chapter: a party that opens every fight with Breach and Rush meets a Completion that rushes the first thing to enter its Zone and Drives it Back; a region whose defining conduct was the hall's orderly evacuation meets a Completion that moves people out of its Rift rather than striking them, one Zone a round, and strikes only what refuses to leave.
4. **The site.** Clearing the Completion exposes where what it held went into the ground: the densest place in the region, with the road and the site as the last two Rifts.
5. **The preparation.** Grade Breakthroughs owns the rest: overcharge, the foundation pill, the check. The site adds density. E-Grade play is the next book's.

**Early arrival and other routes.** A party that reaches the square before the diagram completes finds the region's largest Rift not yet open and the Cordons still falling; there is no Completion to fight yet, and the GM may move the manifestation up rather than make them wait. A party that reaches the cap without clearing the Completion can attempt the Breakthrough anywhere at +0 density, or inside the site's open Rift with the Completion active, which is the hard road. The cap is levels, and the site is a reason, never a gate.

The Completion is nonconscious, as every Imprint is. It has the region's conduct and no one's mind; a construct can reproduce a whole city's protective behavior without acquiring a self, and the chapter says so where a table might wonder.

## Pacing to the cap, rebuilt

From `rules/cultivation.yaml`: a level costs 120 VE; Tolerance is 80 (Mild past 80, Heavy past 160, Critical past 240); Consolidation refines 20 an hour (40 in a high-density region) and a level lands mid-rest; a Peer Kill pays 10, Hard 20, Severe 30, Peak 50, per character; Session Survival 5. Rifts pay the Faction column: 25 / 50 / 90 / 150.

Level 4 to 25 is 21 levels: **2,520 VE.**

**About seventeen sessions at these assumptions, one possible pace.** The GM's dials are the Rift difficulty, the fight count, and the density of the ground the party rests on (40 an hour in a high-density region halves every rest above). **[open]** Whether this whole table prints or the per-band sentence does, with the arithmetic left to Cultivation.

## What to look for: the discovery cadence as guidance

By this band, look for an opportunity to introduce. None of it is scheduled; the play supplies the moment and the GM recognizes it.

- **Band one:** a Hidden Achievement, for something a Rift's four lines did not predict; a first faction contact, which in Halden is Marguerite's list or the hall's invitation; a Saturation crisis, when a Rift's reward lands on a day's kills.
- **Band two:** a Personal Opportunity that tests rather than affirms; the party's first proposal to the System, a Registrar's Standing or anyone's attempt; a paired Opportunity, once the System has recorded the party and an Aru acting together twice.
- **Band three:** the liaison's address (below); a section of the diagram completing where the party can see it.
- **Band four:** the site, seen before it can be used, by a party at 22 that clears the Completion.

## The liaison **[proposal]**

The setting allows the accession liaison to address both worlds through a projection and defers the name and employer until the bar lifts. Proposed rulings for the setting file, then the chapter:

- **The projection carries Aura**, bounded: during the address only, at the gate where it stands, at whatever Grade the liaison holds (C-Grade reads as a regional power in the setting; Aura Resistance for a calm presence is Hard 115). It cannot fight, cannot act on the region, and does not answer questions. The Aura is the glimpse of distant mastery the author direction asks for, and the HRT saves are how the table feels it; the scene is what it says.
- **Its reason to address both worlds:** it announces that the completion of each region's diagram is the region's assessment, and that the accession's terms review at completion. It offers each region one decision the party can be part of: whether the region's Rift clearances are registered as a joint account with its paired Aru region or as separate ones. A joint account is what the paired Opportunities extend; a separate one keeps the region's record its own. The community stances split on it. The address leaves the party with information (when the diagram completes, the region is assessed) and a decision (whose record is it).
- Once. No patron, no second address until the bar lifts.

## The Open Measure bulletin, reader-facing

A lore box in Accession Rifts, in the bulletin's header form (OPEN MEASURE | Subject | Conditions | Sample | By | Replication), reporting one small experiment on an Interceptor at another accession: what was detected (a boundary), what triggered it (the first crossing, never the second while the first was being struck), which intervention worked (a thrown object across the line drew the full committed movement; a person standing still inside the line did not), what remains untested (whether the pattern reacquires by sight or by the boundary), with a failure count.
