# The Lore Pass: Questions to Answer

*A worksheet for Gabriel. Answer under each question in one-line facts. `lore/` is the setting's source of truth the way `rules/` is the numbers' source of truth: the book's lore boxes, the tutorial's hooks, the Initiate's Manual, and the app's System voice all draw from here.*

## How to answer

- **One line per fact.** A fact the app or a chapter can quote. Prose comes later, from the facts.
- **Tag every fact with who knows it:** `[everyone]` (any Integrated adult would know), `[GM]` (true, discoverable in play), `[nobody]` (deliberately unresolved; the mystery is the fact). The tutorial's "What Has Taken Place" already uses this split.
- **Few and deep.** Three to five of anything: races, factions, named powers, events. A fourth race with one sentence is worse than three with a page each.
- **Every System quirk passes the test:** it follows from something true about what the System is, and that truth is discoverable in play. Weirdness a player can eventually explain is the target.
- **Name check.** Every coined name gets checked against the rules vocabulary (Grade, Beat, Zone, Sector, Domain, Principle, Mandate) and against the other names for sound-alikes.
- **Answer Parts A and B first.** They are what the tutorial needs. Parts C through E can follow at their own pace; Part F is Claude's job once the facts exist.

Where a question is already ruled, the ruling is quoted so you are not deciding it twice.

## Part A: What the tutorial needs first

The tutorial is the first place players meet the world, and every hook in it should point at exactly one fact here, so an inspired GM has a thread to pull.

### A1. The alien Initiates

The tutorial fixes these constraints: three to five of them, non-human, entered nineteen days before the party through a different threshold, lived in the Civic Fragment until the Sentinel drove them out, move camp daily, share no language with humans, have a scout who tallies arrivals and cuts arrows for whoever comes next, carry a Greater Healing Pill and two shards, and have one member who wants to fight. Use Pre-System Brigand stats. They can leave through the gate beside the party.

- What race are they, and what is the race's name for itself?
- What is their world like, and what did Integration do to it? Are they a newly Integrated cohort like the humans, or an old race whose young are sent through tutorials?
- Why would a GM's later campaign recognize them: what is this race known for across the sectors? `[everyone]`
- What is one visible trait that marks them at a glance? What is one habit (the tallies, the daily camp moves) that turns out to be cultural rather than tactical?
- What is the scout's name, and what does it want after the gate?
- How do humans hear of this race again? A faction, a market, a war, a rumor?
- What does this race think of humans, before and after this tutorial? Does word travel?

### A2. The valley

- Whose wreckage is it? Name the dead worlds that supplied the arena, the glowing forest, the hanging tower, and the courthouse. One line each. `[GM]`
- Who built the transit nexus and the gate, and does that builder still exist? `[GM]` or `[nobody]`
- What was in the sealed vault behind the arena? Is the answer ever knowable? `[nobody]` is a valid answer, but say so.
- What is the ancient cultivator trapped in the rune matrix: which race, which era, what technique?
- What was the Sorting Machine sorting for, and for whom? What is the Dying Scavenger, and why does its kind die in stages into stones?
- The floating citadel and the sky-sized object seen from the Node: what are they, and do they recur in the campaign?

### A3. The Warden and the dissolution

- What is a System Warden, who built it, and what does a working one do? Are there others, and where?
- What does "administrative overrun" mean in how the System runs sectors? Is dissolution routine or a failure?
- Why are tutorial sectors dissolved at all? What happens to the matter and the energy: is the Recycling Node the answer?
- Is the Warden's wish to escape through the gate a malfunction, or the first sign of something true about Wardens?

### A4. After the gate

- Where does the gate deliver survivors? Name the place. Is it Earth transformed, a neighboring Integrated world, a System hub? This is the first campaign region, so it needs a name, a shape, and three things to do there.
- Which Stinger is the default: the gate onto a landscape, the sky-object, the broadcast Mandate, the alien Initiates leaving, or the higher-Grade entity? If the entity: who is it and what does it want?
- Who else came through gates that day? Do Earth's cohorts land together or scattered across the sectors?

### A5. Earth on Day 1

- What did Integration look like from the ground: the sky, the voice, the first hour? Did governments, networks, and cities survive it?
- Is every human in a tutorial, or only some? What happened to the ones who were not?
- Is Earth now a sector, a world, a tutorial, or a ruin? Can a character go home?
- The survivors' forum (an existing idea): how do humans talk to each other after Integration, and where does a GM say the forum lives?

## Part B: The System

**Already ruled (2026-08-15 and 08-26):** the System is not omniscient; it is fallible and makes mistakes. It measures energy and infers everything else (a sleeping man reads as a corpse). It can be addressed and it wants things, used sparingly. Its history has holes it does not notice, and it recruits players to reconstruct lost knowledge because its limit is embodiment: VE carries the shape of what held it last, the System reads residue but cannot travel to it, it has no hands, and players are the hands. Old sectors run old behavior (version drift). It observes conduct without judging it. **Rejected:** over-affirmation, and any quirk where a reader recognizes the chatbot rather than the world. **Still open:** a closed Title vocabulary that files novel acts under the nearest label; precise about magnitude and vague about kind; unable to say why, since it reports rather than reasons.

- **What does the System want?** One sentence. The Principles chapter's lore box says it exists to discover and refine stable patterns of reality. Is that the whole truth, the System's own account, or a cover story? Tag it.
- What does it want from Earth specifically, and why now? "Prior exemptions: expired" implies Earth was exempt. Exempt from what, and who granted the exemption?
- Who or what made the System, and does anyone know? Is there anything above it?
- How old is it, in a unit people use?
- Are there other Systems, or one System with drifted versions? What does a sector on an old version look like to a player?
- What does Integration physically do to a world? Why do bodies scale ×10 per Grade?
- Why tutorials? What does the System get from a tutorial that it does not get from dropping people into the world?
- Does its voice shift by Grade? (The Initiate's Manual is an F-Grade document and inherits the answer.)
- What does it call itself, if anything? What do the races call it?
- Rule the three open quirks: adopt, cut, or park each.
- What are five things the survivors' forum would list under "the System got this wrong"? Each one must trace to the measures-energy or no-hands facts.

## Part C: The multiverse's shape

### C1. Cosmology and travel

- What is a sector, a world, a threshold, a gate, and a region of high Energy Density? One line each, `[everyone]`.
- How do people travel between worlds? Who controls the gates?
- What is above A-Grade? The tiers above Domain and the Axioms exist in the rules; what do people believe about them? `[nobody]` is fine, but say what the rumors are.
- What time does the multiverse keep? A calendar or an event people count from.

### C2. Races

Three to five prominent Integrated races, `[everyone]`. For each:

- Name (theirs), a look, a homeworld, and one line of what they are known for.
- Their relationship to the System: favored, resentful, dependent, ancient.
- Their attitude to newcomers, and to humans once humans exist.
- One name a GM can drop: a leader, a city, a custom.
- Which of them are the tutorial's aliens (A1)?

### C3. Factions

Three to five factions that cross worlds. The title catalog already names the Iron Court (bureaucratic, grants "Hand of the Iron Court"). For each:

- Name, what it wants, what it offers, what it costs.
- Whether it recruits F-Grade humans, and what it uses them for.
- One person in it a GM can use.

### C4. Named powers

Three to five individuals people talk about: an A-Grade, a Gradebreaker (someone who killed a full Grade up), a villain, a missing legend. For each, what they did, where they are now, and what is true versus rumor.

### C5. History everyone knows

Five events, `[everyone]`, one paragraph each: the first Integration anyone remembers, a war between Integrated worlds, a world lost, a Breakthrough that changed something for everyone, the last time a sector was dissolved with people in it.

### C6. Legends nobody can confirm

Three, `[nobody]`. The kind of thing a found log mentions once.

### C7. Places

A hub where races meet, a frontier where new sectors open, a dead world, a Principle-rich site (the kind that grants "Witnessed by the Mountain"), and the first campaign region from A4. Name, look, who runs it, what a Level 4 party can do there.

## Part D: Texture

- **Aether's sensory identity:** what it feels, smells, and sounds like. Anchors already in the book: VE leaves the dying as pale motes; the first contact is intoxicating (R22).
- What Consolidation feels like from inside. What a Breakthrough feels like. What Aura Pressure feels like at one Grade up, and at three.
- What a Principle feels like when it crystallizes, and what a Domain looks like from outside.
- Naming conventions: how each race names people and places; how the System names things (Sector 7-Alpha, Q-001, M-00).
- What ordinary Integrated life looks like: money, food, sleep, a market, a road.
