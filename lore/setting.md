# Gradebreaker: The Setting

*The setting's source of truth, the way `rules/` is the source of truth for numbers. The book's lore boxes, the tutorial's hooks, the After the Gate chapter, the Initiate's Manual, and the companion app's System voice draw from this file, and no chapter states a setting fact this file does not hold. Prose is written from these facts. This file is never book text.*

## How to read this file

Every statement here is public knowledge unless tagged: any Integrated adult knows it or can learn it from the interface. Four tags narrow that.

- `[GM]`: true, discoverable in play, and kept from players until they find it.
- `[nobody]`: deliberately unresolved. The mystery is the fact, and the book never answers it.
- `[proposal]`: drafted and awaiting Gabriel's ruling. Usable as a placeholder and never quoted in the book.
- `[author]`: Gabriel's direction to the writers. It shapes prose and is never stated in the fiction.

Every coined name is checked against the rules vocabulary (Grade, Beat, Zone, Sector, Domain, Principle, Mandate, Trial, Accord, Aura) and against the other names here for sound-alikes before it enters this file.

## Author direction `[author]`

Gradebreaker is about gaining the freedom to act and then deciding what deserves your power. Its mechanical expression is that abilities become a record of how a character has lived.

The genre experiences the game is built to deliver:

- A frontier settlement grows into a home shaped by the protagonist's choices (Ascend Online; Zac's town in Defiance of the Fall). The System rewards the building, and the rewards are never the reason the character builds. Building a place is one campaign trajectory among several.
- Breakthroughs feel epic. Zac's, and the Eveningtide Asura's C-Grade breakthrough, are the reference.
- Scale stays astonishing. A formidable D-Grade cultivator is negligible beside C-Grade power, and the ladder continues far past that.
- Glimpses of distant mastery arrive early: an axe warrior whose weapon embodies sharpness so completely that looking at it hurts.
- Cleverness sometimes works spectacularly: unusual combinations and unexpectedly strong builds (Ascend Online's vampire-energy build).
- Kindness has intrinsic and relational consequences (Zac's loyalty-producing conduct) and is never a disguised optimal build.

The System causes enmity. People hate it for what it did on Day 1 and are tempted by it at the same time: as a source of personal power, as a new god, as a beacon of higher order. The world is in chaos and rebuilds itself on new principles, out of consorting with the Aru and out of the powers everyone now holds. How civilizations reshape themselves in the Integrated world is the setting's central subject.

Gabriel's own judgment: the System is evil in its indifference. It manufactures misery to acquire knowledge and violates personhood by forcing people into its processes (compare Orom World in Defiance of the Fall). Its opportunities are also real and attractive: a lowly person can acquire mastery, on tractable rules that ordinary life never offered. Characters and cultures reach their own judgments. A deeper purpose may eventually be revealed and none has been chosen; a grand purpose may explain the System's actions without justifying them.

Power grants the freedom to pursue desires. It enables protection or domination, and some abusers face no effective punishment. It cannot buy affection, loyalty, or clarity about existence (Homelander: immense power beside misery and a need to be liked). The highest beings still struggle with meaning.

The multiverse offers extraordinary novelty: alien art, activities, spiritual traditions, places, forms of experience. The setting explores what is universal in sentient life and what is specific to humanity. Advancement answers "what do I do with my life?" in practice without answering it in full. Some things are worth experiencing without a notification.

Earth survives and is retrofitted into the System's multiverse. Old sources of value and authority lose importance; homes, attachments, and inherited institutions keep theirs. People preserve their communities, invent new forms of life, or leave.

Reasonable disagreement is the most compelling conflict (the humans and spiders of Children of Time). Peaceful ingenuity sometimes succeeds, and incompatible needs cannot always be reconciled. The System manufactures conflicts that would otherwise never exist. Scarcity, allocation, misunderstanding, and failed communication among very different sentients all produce conflict. Communities have their own preferences and can disagree with the powerful people protecting them.

Other peoples adapted to Integration as despots, wanderers, protectors, martyrs, and exploiters; some are the teenage hackers of the System's imperfections. Alien species hold varied cultures and politics.

Recognition permits different reactions: resentment of the spying god, relief that one's actions finally matter, self-revision, refusal of the interpretation. The setting prescribes none.

## The System

### Purpose

The System discovers, refines, and preserves patterns through which conscious beings act upon reality. It makes advancement possible and creates the conditions under which people develop and reveal those patterns. This is what its behavior shows at F-Grade and what the Principles chapter's lore box says. Its ultimate purpose, its origin, and any authority above it are `[nobody]`.

### Capability, and why it issues quests

The System transforms worlds, extracts people, manufactures Imprints, and dissolves sectors. It could execute most of what it asks for. It asks anyway, because watching independent cultivators attempt a task, choose, and invent teaches it more than running a known procedure. This preference leaves suffering unresolved when the participants' responses are informative, and the opportunities it offers stay desirable to those same participants. Lost-knowledge quests follow from the same preference: the System wants to see how the knowledge is recovered.

### Fallibility

The System measures energy and infers everything else. It is exact about anything it has priced and approximate about everything else: a sleeping man reads as a corpse at low resolution; a bestiary classification can miss the creature; a Mandate can misdescribe its own objective. Its history has holes it does not notice, and it answers across them with confidence. Old sectors run old behavior: a sector that never received a change runs the version it was built on. A mistake is intelligible in context once its basis is known.

The Hidden Vector Engine is reliable at the table. Fallibility appears in records, procedures, predictions, and local classifications, never as a routine tax on progression. A serious misreading of a character is a deliberate story, rare, and reviewable by the GM.

The System records tendencies. Ruthlessness, caution, generosity, and ambition are described without a moral verdict, and a classification is neither praise nor condemnation. It can reason and sometimes explains. It usually declines a personal audience.

### Death

Energy leaving a being carries information about the being that held it, and death releases what a living subject keeps folded up. The System therefore sometimes favors a particular death, a murder included, for what it will release, and an experienced or unusual being's death draws something like excitement.

Living beings create new information. Death exposes the accumulated pattern completely and ends that person's future discoveries, so the System weighs what a death reveals against what a life would keep producing. A living civilization produces relationships, instruction, experiments, and encounters that extermination would destroy. The System values a person as a continuing source of discovery. That valuation is instrumental and carries no respect for personhood, and the System can misjudge what is worth preserving.

Unusual interest is expressed as a change in attention. A normally terse interface becomes unusually exact. Many fine glyphs orient toward the released motes. A cold classification is followed by an unsolicited observation: *Previously unrecorded interaction identified.* At an exceptional death, witnesses receive more attention than they ever did while pleading for help. There is no kill jingle, no expressed delight, and no sadist persona; what interests the System is what has become available.

This is an occasional incentive and never the explanation of every quest. Institutions have grown up around it; see Mael Torr's archive under The wider world.

### Voice

One impersonal register across every Grade. Higher access changes what the System communicates and never its personality. It states; it never persuades, apologizes, or encourages. Short declaratives, precise numbers, no pronouns for itself. Where a human would soften, it specifies. Its units are the world's: Attributes, levels, Grades, classes, titles, Health, Aether, VE, hours, meters. Rounds, Beats, turns, rolls, dice, Margins, and checks are the table's approximation of the world, and the System never names them; where a rule needs it to convey a game quantity, it states an in-world one the table maps to it.

Humor comes from precision, odd assumptions, and accurate answers inside an appalling frame of reference. The System never quips, never insults for amusement, never misunderstands everything, and carries no chatbot mannerism: no over-affirmation, no flattery, no eagerness to help.

Direct individual conversation is rare. Negotiation happens under exceptional circumstances: a direct demand fails, and a proposal in a format the System recognizes sometimes lands.

Every System notice carries a small mark in its corner, the same one that sat in the corner of the Day 1 message. The System has never named it or explained it; humans call it the clave, and the word is theirs. What it means is `[nobody]`.

The System has no universal name for itself. Each society names it locally. Humans say *the System*, and on the survivors' forum *the Score*. The Aru word renders through Interpretation as *the Assessor*. The Kith word renders as *the Tide*: it comes in, takes, and leaves marks in stone.  Its creator, its age, any higher authority, and whether apparent variation reflects several Systems are `[nobody]`.

### Quirks ruled

The worksheet left three quirks open. Precise about magnitude and vague about kind is adopted as a restatement of measuring energy and inferring the rest. A closed Title vocabulary is cut, since GMs create new titles. Unable to say why is cut, since the System can reason and sometimes explains.

## Accession and Earth

### The order of events on Earth

1. At one moment, every human on Earth heard the System's voice. The words are the Introduction's opening message. In the same moment, about one adult in ten was gone, taken into the System's void and from there into tutorial sectors, with no warning and no goodbye. The taken heard the message in the void. The stayers heard it in place.
2. Some 550 million adults were taken. Cars, aircraft, and operating rooms lost their people at once, and Earth's first-hour dead were counted in that. Most of the taken died in their first hour in a tutorial. Extraction draws only from adults who have reached cultivation readiness; every child stayed.
3. Within the first hour, a System diagram stood in the sky over each city where a paired gate would open: fine, enormous, incomplete, drawn in the interface's own glyph grammar. The first Imprints took bodies beneath it, and the first Accession Rifts opened around where they landed.
4. Gates to Namar opened in a staggered sequence across three days, at culturally or historically significant sites, places of local significance included. A gate could open before its locality had heard of any other. A few hundred pairs.
5. Stayers fought Imprints, cleared Rifts, kept people alive, and leveled. Some acquired considerable experience in three days; many spent the days keeping people alive.
6. Late on Day 3, the tutorial survivors came back through their gates onto Earth, into recognizable parts of their home regions, with Interpretation switched on and the diagram still incomplete above them.

### The diagram in the sky

The diagram is the pattern of an Imprint being drawn before it takes a body. `[GM]` It produces in two ways.

- **The first wave.** In the first hour it drops finished Imprints running archived patterns: the recorded behaviors of dead worlds, the same kind the tutorial's constructs run. These seed the first Accession Rifts. Later waves fall as the region's level band rises. `[GM]`
- **The completion.** The diagram keeps drawing, and what it draws is the region itself: the conduct the System recorded there during accession. When it completes it takes a body, and the region's largest Accession Rift opens. Default: when the party reaches Level 20. The GM moves it. `[GM]`

The Aru see the same diagrams over their gate cities. Regions without a gate have the message, the missing, and the chaos, and few Imprints.

### Earth's condition

Ordinary science and technology persist: computers, radios, chemistry, electricity, engineering. Networks survive unevenly; some regions stay connected and others go dark from damage, missing staff, supply failure, and local danger. Governments and institutions survive as far as their people did. In any region at least one ordinary institution still functions and at least one needs help.

System notices carry broadly intelligible accession terms. There is no System messaging between people; personal messages, missing-person searches, and local news run on surviving networks, radio, couriers, and particular abilities.

The survivors' forum is human-built: surviving or newly hosted pages, relayed by radio and physical copies where the network is gone. It has no single home. Humans and Aru run cables through gates and argue about protocols; the work is part of the setting's novelty.

### Interpretation

Every Integrant of an accession made since the faculty entered the package receives Interpretation at registration; Earth and Namar do. Oren's accession predates it, so Kith do not have it, on Oren or on Earth. It translates a deliberately communicated message, whether speech, sign, or the communicative gestures of an alien language, and conveys what the speaker is trying to say. Nonverbal features that carry the intended message translate with it. Unexpressed thoughts, motives, memories, and the truth of a statement are outside it: a lie translates perfectly. Involuntary emotional displays and cultural implications arrive untranslated, so a person can understand an offer and misunderstand the obligations that come with it.

Interpretation carries both directions of any conversation its holder is party to; one holder is enough. It translates intentional written and recorded messages. It converts no computer protocols and supplies no scientific expertise.

Tutorial sectors built on old versions predate Interpretation. Sector 7-Alpha is one. The party receives Interpretation at registration past the gate, and their first translated conversation with the Kith happens on Earth.

### Cultivation readiness

Children receive Interpretation and perceive System notices. They cannot accumulate or refine VE until their body can sustain cultivated growth without displacing the development still needed for adulthood. The System evaluates each individual against their species and developmental path; it counts no days, tests no intelligence, and equates readiness with nothing about fertility. Human readiness arrives in late adolescence with individual variation. Aru readiness arrives with the second form. A slow-developing species can wait a century.

Readiness is a threshold, and nothing after it revokes eligibility: growth, pregnancy, injury, aging, later metamorphoses. Disability, infertility, and atypical anatomy do not disqualify an adult. Training before readiness is valuable in the ordinary way; no VE is banked from childhood and no catch-up grant arrives. Readiness confers eligibility and nothing else: no legal adulthood, no consent, no fitness to rule.

The System's reason is instrumental: independent development produces varied, enduring cultivators. `[GM]`

### Established worlds during accession

Physical entry to Earth and Namar from established worlds is closed during accession, at every Grade. The paired gates and tutorial returns are the permitted routes. Newcomers may fight and conquer one another; the bar is against established outsiders, remote-controlled forces and imported arsenals included. The bar stays closed throughout the F-Grade campaign, and the GM opens a later phase by an explicit System announcement. An accession liaison addresses both worlds through a projection; a physical visit requires a bounded exception. Name and employer are deferred until the bar lifts. `[GM]` The liaison addresses each paired region once, at its gate, when the region's diagram is near completion. The projection carries the liaison's Aura for the length of the address and nothing else: it cannot act on the region, fight, or answer, and it does not return until the bar lifts. Aura Resistance is the calm value (Hard 115). The address states that the terms of accession are reviewed when the region's diagram completes, and that at review the System publishes the region's record (the conduct it recorded there, the pattern the Completion was drawn from) to every Integrant in the region. Before completion each region lodges one declaration: Rift clearances registered as a joint account with the paired region, or as separate accounts. Anyone the System has recorded clearing a Rift in the region may lodge it, and the last lodged before completion stands; a joint account takes matching declarations from both regions. Joint: a Rift cleared by a mixed group counts for both regions and pays every participant, the two regions receive one published record, and paired Opportunities may address groups. Separate: each region's clearances count for itself, two records are published, and paired Opportunities between recorded individuals continue. The gate, the bar, and the return authorizations are unchanged either way.

## Accession Rifts and Imprints

### Imprints

An Imprint is a nonconscious construct that physically executes a recorded pattern of behavior: a technique, a coordinated maneuver, a defensive response, a bounded repertoire. Its body need not resemble the people who originated the pattern. It contains no copied person, and players can rely on that. The Warden is a different kind of entity; Hessar, below, is a conscious remnant.

Every Imprint is specified in four lines: what it detects, what triggers it, what it does, what stops it. Its behavior is discoverable and reproducible. The same method works under the same conditions until something relevant changes, and an Imprint gains no adaptation without evidence in play. An interceptor attacks the first qualifying target across a visible boundary, completes its committed movement, then reacquires; a decoy creates an opening. Patterns differ between deployments, and shared discoveries keep working.

A defeated Imprint stays defeated. Encounters never reset; a later appearance is a new manifestation. Imprints use the existing combat and reward rules.

### Accession Rifts

An Accession Rift is a bounded site where the System has laid down a pattern and Imprints run it. Three shapes: **Hold** (keep a position for a duration), **Passage** (get people or goods through), **Claim** (take and keep a site or a cache). A prepared Rift states an objective, intelligible boundaries, an activation cue, the Imprints' behavior limits, a completion condition, a reward, and the consequence of leaving it unresolved.

Rifts cluster around gate cities and impose real risk on inhabited places: a pumping station, a road, a hospital. The System chose those circumstances. People pursue Rift rewards willingly and object to the danger imposed on their neighbors at the same time. An encounter's consequences persist: the route is clear, the equipment is accessible, the group is rescued.

Rifts are party-sized problems with concrete local stakes. Humans and Aru compare Rift procedures across the gates and exchange tactics.

A Rift states its reward in the notice and pays the Faction column of the quest reward table at its difficulty (Quests). When a Rift's completion condition is met the System closes it: every Imprint in it stops where it stands and comes apart within the hour into the pale motes of a dissolving sector, fought or not; a Rift solved without a kill pays its reward and no kill VE; a cleared Rift never reopens, and a later Imprint at the site is a new manifestation. The first wave's Imprints run the Interceptor pattern (a boundary, a committed movement, a reset); later waves run the Bearer (a route) and the Cordon (a perimeter); the region's completed diagram runs the region's own recorded conduct. The Bestiary holds the four blocks and After the Gate holds the lines.

### Halden `[GM]`

The book's worked region, fictional, built to be replaced by the GM's own. A river city of about two hundred thousand on both banks of the Halden, joined by one road bridge and one rail bridge. The gate opened on Day 2 in the arch of the old courthouse on Court Square, on the north bank, and the diagram stands over the square. The Flats, south bank: warehouses, the transit depot, the co-op market, the working river; prevailing stance, use; Marguerite Deschamps runs the co-op and pays in food for cleared sites, and Teodor Ilić, a depot dispatcher, will not send drivers into a Rift for anyone's price. Northgate, around Court Square: the hospital, the concert hall, the radio mast on the hill; prevailing stance, worship, centered on the hall's shelter; Pastor Lin Okonkwo's title was granted at the depot fence on Day 2, in front of two hundred people she had led out of the Flats, and Dr. Sefa Aydin, who runs the hospital, wants the hall's people working. The Bend, upriver on the north bank: a residential grid where the street councils formed on Day 1; prevailing stance, refuse; Ana Kowalczyk chaired the first street meeting, and Mike Tran, Level 3 by Day 3, cleared the gym alone and sleeps on the street under a compromise that forbids him to recruit there. The hospital works, short-staffed. The water utility is failing; half its engineers were taken. On Day 3 both bridges are open and three first-wave Rifts stand (the pumping station, the overpass under the rail bridge, the hospital pharmacy), with a fourth Interceptor on the courthouse steps since the first hour, left alone on purpose; the gate in the arch is reached by the side of the building. The second wave puts a Bearer on the road bridge and the quay, so the banks are joined on foot by the rail bridge. The congregation holds its evening vigil at the depot fence, where the title was granted. The Bend's council has met in the courthouse basement since Day 4, by the alley door the Interceptor does not detect, and wants the steps held: while they are, the courthouse is a Rift and nobody's office, and the day it is cleared the customs table moves inside and the port authority has a building in Halden. The Completion takes a body on Court Square; when it comes apart the paving goes with it and opens a shaft into the cut the gate's makers drove under the square, a passage on no map, whose floor is the densest place in the region and the site of the region's Breakthroughs.

**Selan ar Nerava** `[GM]`: second form, a freight dispatcher for the port on the far side of Halden's gate, whose authority treats the gate as a customs problem. Needs four dispatch workers, stranded since Day 2 in the Rift on the freight yard across the gate, whose Imprint answers a three-beat footfall (an Aru walking) and ignores a two-beat one; a human walks in unnoticed, and an Aru carried or wheeled on a freight trolley makes no footfall. Can offer warehouse access on the Namar side and a barge schedule once the river is open. Will not promise cargo that belongs to someone else.

## Aether and Volatile Energy

Aether and VE are one substance in two states. Aether is yours. VE is Aether still wearing the shape of whatever held it last, and Consolidation breaks that borrowed shape down as the energy becomes the cultivator's own.

**Smell and sound.** Aether smells of green sweetness, fresh as leaves crushed between the palms, with a light sweetness that invites another breath. Its source and use add an undertone of scent, sound, or bodily vibration that repeats for that source: a dry smoky finish, a warmer floral sweetness, a musk, a low hum, a rhythm felt in the teeth or the chest. Pick one for a being or a technique and keep it; familiarity lets a character recognize a resemblance before knowing its cause. The associations belong to the particular example and form no universal code.

- Ambient, in a high-density region: the green sweetness returns with each breath, with a soft, clear note heard or felt behind the teeth.
- During Consolidation: the sweetness grows familiar as the foreign undertones fade, and uneven notes beneath the breastbone settle into a low hum that follows the breath.
- At a death: the pale motes bring a brief wash of green sweetness and an uneven chord carrying the trace of the being that died.
- An ordinary Level 1 human notices the signature on Day 1. Perception separates faint details and experience teaches recognition; no Grade is required to begin.

The signature is pleasant even when the circumstances are appalling; at a death it sits beside the body's stillness and the System's sudden attention. On Earth, people call it cut grass before they know what it is. `[proposal]` on the slang. None of this grants a bonus, a range, or a sense stat; a recognized trace is evidence to investigate.

## Temper, weapons, and technology

Temper is an Integrated body's resistance to ordinary force: the energy a weapon carries on its own, whether muzzle energy, mass, an edge, or a fall. What passes through Temper is the wielder's own Force, carried by the weapon. Temper is the Grade multiplier as a bullet feels it: ×1 at F, ×10 per Grade. The F→E Breakthrough is Body Tempering. Temper is lore, with no stat and no track; the rules already express it. Weapons carry no bonus of their own, damage is the Margin, and the Grade Gap Rule adds +100 to the higher-Grade side.

Guns work. A firearm is a firearms-domain weapon: DEX Force, Trained +5, adjacent Zones, one Clash per Beat, damage as Margin. A bullet does what an arrow does in the same hands. On Day 1 that Downs a Level 1 body as well as anything else does; at Level 25 one shot cannot Down a body at the FOR cap; against an E-Grade body it barely lands. Cartridges are the one part of a gun that runs out: a found firearm holds 1d10 rounds, ammunition exists wherever Earth left it, nothing in this book makes more, and an empty gun is an improvised object. Arrows are made and recovered, and the archery and throwing domain covers everything let go, so its Marks are always fed. Over a campaign, Marks go where the ammunition is. Artillery, vehicle weapons, and explosives have no weapon shape the System recognizes: improvised objects, +0, carrying the user's Force and nothing else.

Principle Applications on a projectile (Edge, Distance) apply to bullets as they do to arrows. Cultivated, System-forged firearms exist in the wider universe and are bespoke items.

## The tutorial sector: Sector 7-Alpha

### The sector, the Warden, and the dissolution `[GM]`

Tutorial sectors are maintained environments assembled from the wreckage of dead worlds for observation. Keeping one stable consumes resources. When a sector is retired, dissolution recovers usable matter and energy, and the Recycling Node sorts what is recovered. The Warden is the mobile controller of that final stage: it clears obstructions, breaks down material the boundary cannot process alone, keeps the recovery sequence moving, and executes an assigned evacuation schedule it cannot extend.

Administrative overrun: this sector kept accepting cohorts after the safe completion window for its scheduled retirement, because admission and retirement run on incompatible schedules. The scheduling error explains the overlap. The System's decision to keep the retirement on schedule with people inside is what does the harm.

The damaged Warden has developed a preference for its own continued existence. The gate is a real escape route for it, and it has learned no corresponding concern for anyone else's survival. Its conduct supports the reading and it gives no speech. It is the first small example of something the System produced developing interests the System does not share, and it proves nothing about other constructs.

Sector 7-Alpha runs a version older than Interpretation.

### The four remnants `[GM]`

**The Martial Remnant: Gethra.** The arena belonged to a public martial school in a tradition that preserved failed methods beside victories. The sentries repeat instructional sequences, and the tactical carvings taught students to recognize and counter them; the escalation is a training procedure running without an instructor. Gethra became uninhabitable during the Closed Roads War, in fighting over routes through its region; survivors and successor schools exist elsewhere. The sealed vault holds recordings of high-Grade practitioners' defeats and failed experiments, which surviving schools would pay to suppress or recover. It stays sealed and is lost with the sector; other copies exist. Its door carries a Grade warning in a script the interface does not render.

**The Wild Fragment: Ulassa.** The luminous forest is a transplanted fragment of Ulassa's biosphere, which survived a long climatic decline before the world could no longer support it. Some inhabitants evacuated; some ecosystems were moved. The light waves are a signaling process carried through roots and airborne compounds, used by several species and exploited by others, which is why the blue waves move against the wind. The Snarljaws, the young, the Glow-Stalker, and the trapped creature are living animals in salvaged habitat. The vast passing creature is an independent, uninterested presence. The den keeps its reward inside a harmless young animal.

**The Arcane Debris: Veyr.** Veyr's institutes studied how techniques could be preserved and taught across radically different bodies. A research disaster propagated through interconnected facilities; emergency measures arrested parts of the collapse without restoring habitability, and the hanging tower is one arrested pocket. Its records hold teaching matrices, damaged defenses, and effects still running after their authors died. The Edge, Pulse, and Resonance shards, the technique chamber that lends a technique, the data altar's one truthful answer, and the observation deck's view of the present purge stay as the tutorial writes them. The Fragment Wraith is a broken defensive effect with a fixed territory.

**The Civic Fragment: Pellam.** Pellam was a trading world made prosperous and vulnerable by its transit network. During the Closed Roads War its authorities converted civic buildings into evacuation offices, shelters, and command posts, and this building served all three. Its records concern claims to passage, missing people, requisitions, and disputed emergency orders. The medical facility and the Husk Sentinel were meant to preserve a working refuge; a later change in authorization made the Kith intruders, an obsolete instruction producing a cruel outcome. The tribunal's binding decisions operate through the building's jurisdiction and enforcement, and a ruling can be binding and unjust. The Kith's records are recent additions to an ancient facility.

**Hessar and Common Bearing.** The fragment of mind in the rune matrix is Hessar, a Surn teacher from Veyr. Their last technique, Common Bearing, distributed a burden among consenting supports; during the collapse Hessar used it to hold an evacuation structure together, and the matrix holds their last conscious fragment. Hessar was an impatient, ambitious teacher who made one extraordinary choice. The tutorial's three outcomes stand: accepting the technique memory pays the Accord recognition, harvesting ends the remnant in distress for the listed VE, freeing it allows the dissolution. The transfer is a **technique memory**.

**The Sorting Machine.** It sorts by whether an object was ever owned by something that died here. It is a field collector for a salvage archive, which compares released energy with the tools, habits, and techniques of the dead before bulk recycling. It classifies and does nothing else. The System's interest in death has mundane institutional consequences as well as sinister ones.

**The stonefold.** The Dying Scavenger is a stonefold, a living scavenger species. At the end of life it concentrates its remaining usable energy into a mineral core, deliberately and in stages. A stone taken before the animal finishes holds what was already moved into it; killing the animal spoils the stone; a finished core comes away warmer, and abandoned cores go cold within days. The stone holds energy and nothing of the animal's mind. The animal wants to finish.

**The nexus, the Spindlehouse, and the cradle.** The transit nexus is System-provisioned infrastructure of standardized modules, repaired over many cycles by different crews; its spiral serves recovery and routing. The floating citadel is a disused maintenance settlement, a Spindlehouse in transit workers' speech: workshops, stores, and quarters on platforms, empty, out of the party's reach, lost with the sector. The sky-sized object is a recovery cradle that receives sorted material during retirement, machinery whose function is hard to infer at human scale. The Stinger's living sky-object is a different thing and stays a GM alternative.

### The Kith of Oren

The tutorial's non-human Initiates are Kith, from Oren, an older Integrated world. Narrow upright bodies; two long outer arms and a smaller pair held close beneath the ribs; sensitive folds from the jaw down the neck that register vibration. Their speech combines voice and deliberate gesture. Familiarity teaches a human to read the expressions and involuntary movements that are not language. Their anatomy grants no extra actions.

Oren has inhabited escarpments and tidal basins. Built stone carries vibration for work, warning, and art. Basin cities, traveling households, solitary cultivators, and religious communities disagree about how to live. Kith are known along certain trade routes for practical work in resonant stone and structures, a reputation tied to schools and exports. Integration there is old enough that no community keeps a pre-System government unchanged, and Oren's accounts disagree about whether mutual refuge or extraordinary protectors mattered more in its early crises.

**How they came to be there.** `[GM]` The four accepted a System commission to survey and salvage the old sector, bringing scouting, supplies, fighting, and repair; the offer said nothing about confinement with later arrivals. Once inside, the System registered them as participants whose departure depended on the sector's tutorial release, and left them there while admission and retirement ran on their separate schedules. At the evacuation it granted the four an exception to enter Earth with the human cohort: these four, their inspected carried equipment, and nothing else, with no right for anyone else to claim the same by entering a tutorial. The evidence exists in play: the original offer, the participant registration, the exception notice. Why the System wanted four Kith in a human tutorial at all, and what it hoped to see, is `[nobody]`.

The group is four by default; the tutorial allows three to five, on Pre-System Brigand statistics.

- **Kes**, the scout. Wants an onward route and, eventually, a share in a traveling workshop. Leaves marks for strangers because that is ordinary responsible work in a traveling household; the arrival tallies and the arrow are a household's travel account. The daily camp moves are tactics. `[GM]` The four lived in the Civic Fragment sixteen days; the Sentinel drove them out three days before the party arrives, and Kes has gone back each day since to cut one more mark on the tally by the door.
- **Nemi**, supplies. Carries the Greater Healing Pill and spends it only against a known danger.
- **Tovan**, the fighter. Wants the shelter the Sentinel took back, and resents a shrinking range of choices.
- **Sava**, the mechanic. Carries the two shards and is interested in human tools; may want to stay on Earth for a while.

They judge particular humans by experience. The Kith exit the tutorial gate beside the party and arrive on Earth with them late on Day 3, with the party's Interpretation working for the first time.

**What each believes on Day 3**, in their own words, adjusted to what the party actually did. Kes: "I saw you before you found each other; I want each name beside what each person did." Nemi: "You are still finding out what your bodies can survive; I want to see the wound before anyone spends the pill." Sava: "I watched you make that tool do another job; show me what it was built to do." Tovan: "You agreed who went through first while the ground was going; who gets to refuse the order next time?"

**What each wants in the first week.** Kes offers to scout an onward route for supplies and a written account of the work, the kind of account a traveling workshop on Oren would weigh. Nemi asks to inspect a proposed camp and its escape route before committing the group's remaining medicine, and refuses a promise that amounts to "we will find somewhere later." Sava offers to examine a damaged structure beside a human repairer in exchange for time at the repairer's bench, and wants a reason to stay that includes work of their own. Tovan offers to stand watch through the others' first rest if someone will then walk the roads with them, and refuses to let a host's protection become a prohibition on leaving. Anything spent in the tutorial stays spent.

**Going home.** At registration on Earth each Kith receives an individual System authorization for return to Oren, usable at a paired gate whenever the bearer chooses. It carries the bearer and their possessions to a maintained receiving point on Oren, admits no companion, and opens no passage back. It costs no VE and demands no service; its cost is every Earth relationship that cannot be continued across the accession bar. Declining carries no penalty, and the authorization stays open throughout accession. After departure, the bar keeps that Kith on Oren until it lifts.

**How word reaches Oren.** `[GM]` A returning Kith carries the first account in person, with Kes's marks or a reconstruction of them. The receiving household can lodge a copy with a Wayhouse, whose travelers carry it on as a named witness's travel account. Open Measure can publish the tests and observations in it with the witness, conditions, and gaps attached; a bulletin about one human party warrants no conclusion about humans.

**A household receives its traveler.** In these Kith's traveling household, a returning member is fed and given a place to sleep before the route account is opened on the table and the household asks which absent people must be told that they survived. Other households do otherwise.

**A camp scene**, optional and unrewarded: at a quiet stop a Kith taps and rubs a stone slab or a metal rail, then invites a human to rest a palm on it. The human feels a simple repeating rhythm and misses the frequencies and spatial distinctions the Kith hears. The performer changes the pattern when a second hand touches the surface and shows one part the human can reproduce. On Oren the communal version fills a room.

## Namar and the Aru

### Namar

Namar is a newly integrating inhabited world whose most numerous people call themselves the Aru. Its cities are cities: homes, factories, public institutions, freight, entertainment, neglected districts, places people love. It has its own scientific and industrial history. Many urban regions run wired optical networks; others use radio; some are poorly connected. An Aru communications engineer understands what a human cable is for and finds no compatible socket. Each species' technical choices make sense under its own materials, institutions, and history.

Three first contacts, as local reactions: a port authority treats its gate as a customs problem; an inland mutual-aid group treats it as a route to supplies; an armed coalition treats it as a vulnerability or an opening. Earth shows the same range.

Most inhabited regions of Namar support human life without suits or life support, and Earth supports Aru the same way; air, gravity, and climate were part of the System's compatibility assessment. Local food is a separate question.

The System chose Earth and Namar for each other. `[GM]` It assessed environmental compatibility and the two populations' capacity to interact and learn from their differences. It wants sustained, informative interaction, and the suitability that enables cooperation enables conquest. Humans and Aru infer the care behind the selection from evidence.

### Aru bodies

Aru have three weight-bearing legs, two manipulating arms, and movable hearing fans along the head. An adult stands about 2.1 meters at the head, taller than most humans. The torso is upright and stands over the legs, and the three legs splay wide, so an Aru takes up a great deal of floor. The build is lean and bony: long limbs, visible joints, and smooth hairless skin the color of pale bone.

Each leg ends in a blunt three-toed foot. The arms are longer in proportion than a human's and fold at two elbows apiece, in a shallow Z, and the hands have five fingers. The head is long and tapering, carried on a short neck low between the shoulders, with small dark eyes set wide and a mouth that closes in a plain line.

The fans are two broad flat blades spreading to either side of the head and slightly back, together wider than the shoulders, ribbed into panels, with a hearing opening at the base of each. Deliberate fan movements are part of language and translate; involuntary ones a human learns to read.

Anatomy shapes furniture, gait, and comfort, and it shapes dress. Aru garments hang or wrap from the shoulders in wide panels to mid-leg, over harnesses and belts that carry tools and pouches. A three-legged body takes no trousers, and the legs go bare. Nothing covers the head, where the fans are. Aru wear Aru clothing; a human garment fits nothing on them.

Aru live through four discrete bodily forms. Each transition brings a period of shelter, reduced mobility, and tissue remodeling. They remember their earlier lives and remain the same people.

| Form | Body | Life |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First** | Juvenile: smaller, more flexible, large hearing fans; the future arms support early crawling. No sustained cultivation. | Children learn, work at their tasks, and form relationships. |
| **Second** | First adult body. Cultivation readiness arrives here. Reproductive organs are not yet functional. | Varek is in this form. |
| **Third** | Reproductive capacity, a broader torso, folding plates that shelter an external clutch. Any fertile adult can fertilize and any can brood; reproduction takes a partner. | Care arrangements vary by community and household. |
| **Fourth** | Reproductive capacity recedes; brooding structures shrink; the hearing fans divide into layered lobes. Cultivation continues. | Another adult form. Age and power stay distinct. |

Transitions have variable timing. The two adult transitions erase no levels; refinement accommodates the new body.

Aru physiology supplies no male and female binary, and gender systems vary by culture. Varek's community recognizes masculine, feminine, and ungendered identities independent of form or brooding. Varek introduces himself in a masculine category and is *he* in the story. Interpretation renders what a person communicates about themselves and infers nothing about anatomy.

### Aru names

A widespread formal convention: personal name, form particle, household name. The personal name persists through life; the particle changes at each transition; household membership is social and changes by adoption, partnership, migration, or choice.

| Element | Convention |
| --- | --- |
| Personal name | Two syllables in transcription: Varek, Selan, Iver, Neris. Names encode neither gender nor virtue. |
| Form particle | **el** first, **ar** second, **ir** third, **or** fourth. |
| Household name | Two or three syllables: Soval, Nerava, Telun. |
| Address | Personal name alone unless disambiguation helps. |
| Example | **Varek ar Soval**: Varek, second form, of the Soval household. After his next transition, Varek ir Soval. Same person, same obligations. |

### First contact: Alishba and Varek

At an early gate in Pakistan, Alishba, who repairs household appliances, met Varek ar Soval, who maintains public water equipment, while people elsewhere were still working out what had happened. Neither held office, commanded anyone, or was chosen. She spoke Urdu, he spoke his own language, and Interpretation carried both. Their first agreement was small: neither would attack, each would say what they knew, and each would help the people on their own side. Their first task together was retrieving a person stranded by the gate's appearance and marking a safe approach. Their friendship started an alliance without making either of them an ambassador.

The meeting is apocryphal. Human and Aru accounts agree on the gate, the two names, and the stranded person. They disagree about who reached out first, what was said, and who took the risk, and both sides embellish. The book tells the two accounts side by side. Which details the accounts dispute is `[GM]`; which account is right is `[nobody]`.

**The deed.** The first wave had put an Accession Rift in the one door between the worlds: interceptor Imprints on both sides of the gate's plane, each attacking the first thing to cross a boundary, completing a committed movement, then reacquiring. Each side could see only its own interceptors, and a crossing had to be timed to both sides' committed movements at once, so the pattern could be solved only by two people counting together across the gate. On the second day Alishba and Varek worked it out by shouting across the plane, then led a mixed crowd through in one timed passage: the first Rift cleared by humans and Aru together, and the first gate anyone on either world could walk through freely. The clearance is documented on both sides, and the two accounts agree on it.

**Ambassadors.** After the clearance they were the two people on either side who could get a thing done across the gate: a message carried, a stranded relative found, a tactic passed on. In a world where a message between worlds travels by a person walking through a gate, they were the channel. Unofficial for weeks, then official: the authorities on each side appointed the one person the other side already trusted. Their standing is real and unenforceable. Neither can promise what a government, an armed group, or a frightened neighbor will do, and each has been disowned by their own side at least once and reinstated when nothing else worked.

**The System's part.** `[GM]` The System chose the pairing for sustained, informative interaction, and the two of them are the first instance of what it wanted. It issues them **paired Opportunities**: a Personal Opportunity addressed to both at once, the same offer in two logs in two languages, completing only if both act. A paired Opportunity affirms or tests the friendship the way an ordinary one affirms or tests a pattern: carry this across; refuse this for the other side's sake; tell the truth when it costs your own side. Paired Opportunities go to any human and Aru whose conduct the System has recorded together, so player characters who befriend Aru receive them, and Alishba and Varek are the precedent the book names. As a quest shape it extends Personal Opportunities, which the Quests chapter addresses to one behavioral signature, by addressing one to two signatures from issue; the Quests chapter gains that line when After the Gate lands.

## The wider world

Established background. None of it needs to appear in the F-Grade book; the Iron Court is the one name the book already uses.

### The Surn

Segmented, upright beings with a broad supporting base and fine manipulating tendrils around the upper torso. They use deliberate surface-color changes with motion for language, and Interpretation translates it. Veyr hosted several Surn communities among many peoples, and living Surn populations elsewhere give Veyr descendants and critics.

### Cross-species bonds

Bonds begin with specific people discovering shared practices: companionship, cohabitation, chosen kinship, mutual care, work, ritual. Attraction and reproduction need not align with those bonds or exist in corresponding forms. Whether music, humor, monogamy, or any family structure is universal is a question inhabitants can investigate.

### Three people whose power matters

- **Istren**, C-Grade protector of an Oren basin polity. Istren prevented its destruction and later secured the routes its people survived by. Centuries of protection became a constitutional problem: the emergency authority never lapsed. Some citizens love them; others want the right to take risks Istren forbids. Insightful, generous, and unable to let go.
- **Mael Torr**, D-Grade patron of a death archive. Torr buys access to terminal cultivators' final releases and accumulated records. Some arrangements are voluntary and enrich families; others run on debt, confinement, and calculations about when a subject is worth more dead. Torr exploits the System's information economy and speaks for no one else. Torr enters stories through brokers and contracts long before a party could face the patron. Species undesignated.
- **Bel Sar**, missing route explorer. Their travel accounts connect inhabited places no published route reaches. Reports disagree about whether they died, found a place worth staying, or escaped the obligations their reputation carried. `[nobody]` on their fate. A surviving observation is useful whatever became of its author.

None of the three needs to appear in the F-Grade book.

### Calendar

Traders count years in **Toll Reckoning**, from an old route-access agreement, with local calendars alongside. Days are not simultaneous across worlds. No book text carries a date until a plot depends on one.

### Places

- **Latchwater**, a gate harbor: warehouses, lodging, courts, repair yards, and constituencies fighting over fees and access. Wayhouse shelter and Open Measure notices are found there. Low-Grade work: recovering freight, escorting crews, checking abandoned routes, protecting a repair. Reachable from Earth only after the established-worlds bar lifts.
- **The paired gates** are the first frontier. Each joins two settled places.
- **The Listening Scar**, on Oren: a long rock fracture where vibrations persist and interact unusually. Schools argue over what can be learned there; some visit to cultivate, some to hear a performance, some to remember the dead. It grants nothing on its own.

### Terms

A **world** is an inhabited or once-inhabited celestial home: Earth, Oren, Namar. A **sector** is a System-administered region or constructed environment, and need not be a whole planet; the tutorial's sub-areas are also called sectors in the GM text. A **gate** is a maintained passage between two locations. A **threshold** is a crossing or transition. Energy Density keeps its rules meaning. Above A-Grade is `[nobody]` for this release.

### The Uncrowned

A Gradebreaker is someone who has killed a being a full Grade above them. The achievement inspires admiration because the gap is ordinarily overwhelming, and it is no institution. One legend: a captive brought out for public execution killed the ruler who had sentenced them, from a full Grade below. Admirers call the survivor the Uncrowned. Training halls copy the stance from conflicting depictions; travelers argue about the weapon and where the survivor went. The method, the circumstances, and the true name are `[nobody]`.

### In-world sources

The book quotes a small set of recurring in-world documents, as chapter epigraphs, lore boxes, and asides. Each has one voice, defined here, and each has one characteristic way of being wrong. An epigraph is true by default and carries one fact; a wrong one appears only where the chapter's GM text names the error, so the GM always holds the truth. Atmosphere on its own is cut.

- **The Gethra Accession Ledger.** The oldest account of an Integration in its scholarly tradition, copied by later schools; the copies disagree. Voice: old, dry, counted. Wrong by transmission: a number differs between copies. Gethran successor schools on Oren and at Latchwater hold teaching copies, with later annotations kept visibly separate; no copy is the authoritative original.
- **Open Measure bulletins.** Reproducible accounts of techniques, Imprint behavior, and failed experiments, with a byline (Ensa and others). Voice: method, conditions, sample size, negative results. Wrong by scope: a result that held under its conditions, quoted beyond them. A bulletin's header: OPEN MEASURE | Subject | Conditions (site, Grade, sector version) | Sample (subjects, attempts, failures) | By | Replication. Failures and "not tested" stay in the report.
- **The survivors' forum.** Humans from Day 3 on, comparing notes on the System. Voice: plain, urgent, personal, the found-log register. Wrong the way people are wrong: confident, partial, occasionally exactly right. Its contributors call the linked copies **Still Here**; the book's attribution stays *the survivors' forum*.
- **Bel Sar's route accounts.** Travel writing by a missing author. Voice: places, distances, what a road costs. Wrong by age: the road has changed. An undated extract is labeled undated.
- **The System.** Its own notices, the sanctioned omniscient voice. Wrong by inference: exact about what it measured, mistaken about what it inferred.

### Sensory direction `[author]`

Departing VE motes draw a specific, unsettling change in System attention. Consolidation feels like discovering that a familiar movement now organizes the whole body differently. A Breakthrough makes a once-dangerous effort feel ordinary. Aura Pressure registers as bodily certainty about another being's capacity, interpreted through each character's own senses. A Principle is felt through a concrete perception before its label: an edge, a recurring rhythm, a relation. A Domain makes some aspect of the surroundings behave according to the cultivator's power, within the Domain rules. Aether anchors already in the book: VE leaves the dying as pale motes, and first contact is intoxicating.
