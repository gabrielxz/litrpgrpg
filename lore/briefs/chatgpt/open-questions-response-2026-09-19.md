# Gradebreaker: responses to the three lore questions

Handoff version for Gabriel and Claude, 2026-09-19. Gabriel has selected **green sweetness** as Aether's smell, with a pleasant common signature and recognizable variations by source and use across more than one sense. Section 1 supplies the finished write-up of that direction. Other additions remain proposals unless explicitly identified as established; none has been inserted into `setting.md`. `[GM]` means discoverable in play and `[nobody]` preserves an unresolved question. Untagged proposed facts would become public if adopted. The sensory comparisons describe human perception.

The detailed brief asks for more than five facts under its Kith and source questions; the three-to-five limit is applied to each requested subtopic.

## 1. What Aether smells and sounds like

### Facts for the setting

- **Common signature:** Aether smells of green sweetness, fresh as leaves crushed between the palms with a light sweetness that invites another breath; its source and use can add a recognizable undertone of scent, sound, or bodily vibration.
- **Ambient, high density:** The green sweetness returns with each breath, accompanied by a soft, clear note that can be heard or felt faintly behind the teeth.
- **Consolidation:** The green sweetness becomes familiar as the foreign undertones fade, and uneven notes beneath the breastbone settle into a low hum that follows the cultivator's breathing.
- **A death:** The pale motes bring a brief wash of green sweetness and an uneven chord carrying a trace of the being that died, recognizable through repeated encounters with that trace.
- **First perception:** An ordinary Level 1 human can notice the signature on Day 1; Perception separates faint details, and experience teaches recognition, with no higher Grade required to begin.

### Source, use, and refinement

Aether and VE are the same substance in different states. VE still carries the pattern of its former holder; Consolidation breaks that borrowed pattern down as the energy becomes the cultivator's own. The familiar green sweetness runs through both states. Refinement changes the accompanying trace from something foreign and tangled into something settled and familiar.

Different sources and practiced uses can carry their own recurring accents: a dry, smoky finish, a warmer floral sweetness, a musky undertone, a low hum, or a particular rhythm felt in the teeth or chest. Pick a trace for the particular being or technique and repeat it consistently. Familiarity lets a character recognize a resemblance before they know its cause. These associations belong to the individual example; they are not a universal code in which every protective technique hums or every cutting technique rings.

The common signature is pleasant even when the circumstances are appalling. At a death, its appeal can sit beside the body's stillness and the System's sudden attention to what has been released. The unsettling part need not come from making Aether smell foul.

These sensations introduce no bonus, detection range, or new sense stat. A recognizable trace is evidence to investigate, not automatic identification, access to memories, or proof of a cause of death. Use the sensory channels a character perceives; following a clue never requires both smell and hearing. The high-density cue accompanies the established F-Grade refining rate of 40 VE an hour rather than 20 and changes no rule.

### Read-aloud examples

**First contact or a high-density region:**

> You draw a breath and catch a green sweetness, fresh as leaves crushed between your palms. You breathe in again. A soft, clear note seems to hang behind your teeth.

**Consolidation:**

> The green sweetness returns as your breathing settles. There are other traces tangled through it, becoming fainter with each breath. Beneath your breastbone, uneven notes draw together into a low, familiar hum.

**A death:**

> Pale motes lift from the body. A wash of green sweetness reaches you, carrying a dry, smoky trace, and a brief, uneven chord sounds behind your teeth. The body is still.

The smoky trace is an example to replace with the particular being's established signature. Smell alone supplies no conclusion about its powers, temperament, or death.

## 2. The Kith after the tutorial

### Why they are there, and why Earth admits them

**Established text:** `setting.md` explicitly leaves their routing into a human tutorial `[nobody]`. They entered through another threshold nineteen days earlier, did not expect confinement, and escape onto Earth beside the party. Tutorial returns are named among the permitted routes through the accession bar, but the setting supplies no reason these four established-world inhabitants qualify.

**Recommended explanation, wholly proposed:** The System treats a small group of displaced workers as useful participants and then values the relationships they formed enough to admit them together. Adopting this explanation would replace the existing `[nobody]` entry with discoverable `[GM]` facts.

- `[GM]` The four accepted a System-issued survey-and-salvage commission in the old sector, bringing scouting, supplies, fighting ability, and repair skills; the offer did not disclose that they would be confined with later arrivals.
- `[GM]` Once inside, the System registered them as participants whose departure depended on the sector's tutorial release, leaving them trapped while admission and retirement schedules continued to run independently.
- `[GM]` At evacuation the System deliberately grants these four named survivors an exception to enter Earth with the human cohort, because it wants to observe whether relationships formed under confinement persist when the survivors can choose their own work and companions.
- `[GM]` The exception admits these F-Grade individuals and their inspected carried equipment only, with no accompanying force, established-world resupply, remote-controlled agent, or right for another traveler to claim the same exception by entering a tutorial.
- `[GM]` A route back to Oren becomes available after Earth registration; the System chose Earth as the shared discharge point despite being capable of sending the Kith home, and each Kith can then accept or decline the separate return offer.

**The security judgment:** Their information and skills can change events on Earth. The System accepts that risk for the encounters it can observe. Four people at the tutorial's scale, unable to bring in patrons, armies, or equipment, cannot exercise the same kind of established-world intervention that the bar excludes. Their entry is a deliberate, individually bounded exception at F-Grade; low Grade alone never grants eligibility. No claim of perfect loyalty screening or harmlessness is needed.

This makes the System responsible for the choice. The Kith can resent both the confinement and being sent somewhere they never agreed to visit. It also gives the party discoverable evidence: the original work offer, the participant registration, and the later exception and return notices. The System's ultimate purpose remains unresolved.

### What the four believe on Day 3

These are individual readings of the people they met. Use the actual tutorial record: Sava's line below assumes a witnessed improvisation, and includes an alternative if there was none. None is a judgment on humanity as a species.

- **Kes:** "I saw you before you found each other; I want each name beside what each person did."
- **Nemi:** "You are still finding out what your bodies can survive; I want to see the wound before anyone spends the pill."
- **Sava:** "I watched you make that tool do another job; show me what it was built to do." If nobody improvised: "You carried that tool through the gate; show me what made it worth the weight."
- **Tovan:** "You agreed who went through first while the ground was going; who gets to refuse the order next time?"

### What they want in the first week

- **Kes** offers to scout an onward route in exchange for supplies and a written account of the work, something a traveling workshop on Oren could use when considering Kes for a share.
- **Nemi** asks to inspect the proposed camp and its escape route before committing the group's remaining medicine to a joint expedition, and refuses a promise that amounts to "we will find somewhere later."
- **Sava** offers to examine a damaged structure with a human repairer in exchange for time at the repairer's bench, and wants a reason to stay that includes work of their own.
- **Tovan** offers to stand watch through the others' first rest if someone will then walk the available roads with them, and refuses to let a host's protection become a prohibition on leaving.

If the Greater Healing Pill or either shard was spent in the tutorial, it stays spent. These requests never replenish the inventory. A hostile history changes whom the Kith will approach; the lines do not require reconciliation.

### Returning to Oren

- At registration on Earth, each Kith receives an individual System authorization for return to Oren, usable at a paired gate once the bearer chooses to depart.
- Activation gives that bearer a temporary outward passage to a maintained receiving point on Oren; the authorization admits no companion and opens no passage from Oren into Earth or Namar.
- The passage charges no VE, demands no service, and permits the bearer's carried possessions; its cost is leaving behind every Earth relationship that cannot be continued across the accession bar.
- Declining or dismissing the offer carries no penalty, leaves the Kith free to remain on Earth, and leaves the authorization available throughout accession; after departure, the entry bar prevents a return visit.
- The return authorization establishes a way home independently of whether the proposed original commission and admission exception above are adopted; until that ruling, the original routing remains unresolved in the source setting.

**Design note:** This is a proposed, explicitly limited repatriation procedure. It needs to be added to the accession rules alongside the existing paired-gate and tutorial-return routes. It gives no human access to an established world and creates no trade corridor during the F-Grade campaign. The choice to stay can be reconsidered; the choice to leave separates the characters until the bar lifts.

### How word reaches Oren

- `[GM]` A returning Kith carries the first account from this group in person, including Kes's surviving marks or a reconstruction that labels what was remembered; if none returns, this group's account does not reach Oren by that route.
- `[GM]` The receiving household can lodge a copy with a Wayhouse, whose travelers carry it through established-world routes as a named witness's travel account, including the help received and the obligations claimed.
- `[GM]` Open Measure can publish tests and observations from that account with the witness, conditions, and gaps attached; a bulletin about the human party's method never warrants a conclusion about every human.

### A household receives its traveler

- In these Kith's traveling household, a returning member is fed and given a place to sleep before the route account is opened on the table and the household asks which absent people must be told that they survived.

This is one household's practice. Other Oren households may welcome, question, celebrate, or resent a return differently.

## 3. The in-world sources

The five-source set remains a proposal in `setting.md`. I recommend adopting it and keeping its already specified error modes. The reader should recognize a source by the work its writer is doing: counting, testing, asking for help, planning passage, or administering.

### The Gethra Accession Ledger

- **Maker, reader, object:** Gethran accession recorders compiled the original entries; successor teachers and copyists preserve them in bound codices used by pupils, historians, and institutions asserting continuity with Gethra.
- **Age:** Its opening entries belong to Gethra's accession, before the Closed Roads War dispersed the surviving schools; it is the earliest accession account preserved by that scholarly tradition, with no year fixed for the book.
- **Copies:** Gethran successor martial schools on Oren and at Latchwater hold teaching copies, with later annotations visibly separated from the accession entries; no surviving copy is designated the authoritative original.
- **Voice, sample:** **Old, dry, counted.** "The failed methods were entered beside the victories."
- **Error and visible tell:** Transmission changes a count; when a disputed count matters, the excerpt includes a copyist's note giving the other reading, and the adjacent GM text identifies the actual error without changing any rule value.

The proposed holders are school traditions and locations, rather than newly named institutions. Later material, such as a comment on the Rill Closure, must be attributed to a later annotation, never silently placed in an accession-era writer's mouth.

### Open Measure bulletins

- **Maker, reader, object:** Practitioners such as Ensa sign reproducible reports for other practitioners, teachers, and travelers; copies circulate between contributors and are posted at Wayhouses, including Latchwater.
- **Header:** `OPEN MEASURE | Subject: [test] | Conditions: [site, Grade, relevant sector version] | Sample: [subjects; attempts; failures] | By: [author] | Replication: [status]`.
- **Voice, sample:** **Conditional, measured, signed.** "An interceptor completes its committed movement before reacquiring a target."
- **Error and visible tell:** A quoted result is extended beyond its tested conditions; preserve the header and put the conflicting condition beside the quotation, so the GM can locate the invalid inference in the excerpt itself.
- **Negative results:** Failures and untested conditions stay in the report, including an explicit "not tested" where a reader might otherwise infer a result.

The header is a form, with placeholders for actual observations. Do not invent sample sizes merely to give an epigraph a scientific appearance.

### The survivors' forum

- **Maker, reader, object:** Humans compare their own experiences for other survivors from Day 3 onward through surviving or newly hosted pages, with posts relayed by radio and copied onto paper wherever connections fail.
- **Local name:** Contributors call their linked copies **Still Here**; a quoted post retains its local host and author when known, and an anonymous or relayed post is labeled accordingly.
- **Where it lives:** The GM places an accessible copy on an existing local network or in a particular relay keeper's hands; there is no central server whose loss erases the whole forum, and no System messaging service carries it.
- **Voice, sample:** **Plain, urgent, personal.** "A lie comes through Interpretation perfectly; you still have to decide whether to believe the person."
- **Error and visible tell:** A contributor generalizes from a partial view; an excerpt used for a mistaken claim includes the limiting observation or a correcting reply, such as a claim that every network failed followed by the location of a working one.

The book's attribution remains *the survivors' forum*, as requested in the epigraph brief. Still Here is an optional in-world name, pending approval.

### Bel Sar's route accounts

- **Maker, reader, object:** Bel Sar wrote route notebooks for travel; surviving sheets and copies pass among explorers, freight crews, and displaced travelers who need to know where a road leads and what passage demands.
- **Voice, sample:** **Ground, passage, cost.** "At Latchwater, shelter can be found through the Wayhouse Compact."
- **Dating:** Each usable extract preserves the last known journey or route condition; an undated copy is labeled undated, and the book supplies no calendar date until a plot needs one.
- **Error and visible tell:** A road has changed since the account was written; when an old instruction is wrong, a later traveler's annotation or a present obstacle appears in the same excerpt, making the changed condition visible.
- `[nobody]` Possessing an authentic route account settles nothing about whether Bel Sar died, stayed somewhere, or left deliberately.

### System notices

- **Maker, reader, object:** The System issues notices in the recipient's interface; people can repeat or transcribe a notice, but their copy is separate from the private interface that displayed it.
- **Voice, sample:** **Brief, exact, impersonal.** "Physical entry from established worlds: closed during accession."
- **Error and visible tell:** A notice can misclassify what an energy measurement signifies; whenever the book quotes such an error, it supplies contradictory observable evidence beside it, such as a corpse classification beside the sleeping subject's breathing.
- **Reliability:** The GM identifies the inference that failed; measured quantities, priced rewards, and the normal operation of character progression retain their stated rules.

### Does a sixth source earn a place?

**None as a recurring epigraph source.** A Wayhouse ledger overlaps with the route accounts and can be quoted within their annotations; a Pellam account also belongs among route documents and found records. A Mael Torr contract earns a specific handout when its obligations matter in play, but making it a recurring narrator would give the death archive disproportionate space in the F-Grade book. Keep all three available as occasional documents.

### How an error can be legible from the text alone

A confident tone cannot establish that a statement is false. The excerpt needs a visible discrepancy: a variant reading, a breached condition, a correction, a later annotation, or an observation that contradicts the classification. The GM text then states the truth. None of the epigraph candidates in the companion draft requires an unmarked falsehood.
