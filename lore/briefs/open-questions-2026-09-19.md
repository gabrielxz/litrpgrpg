# Gradebreaker lore: three open questions

*A brief for ChatGPT. The base is `lore/setting.md`, the setting's source of truth; everything below extends it and nothing below may contradict it. Answer in one-line facts a chapter or the app can quote. Tag a fact `[GM]` when players should discover it rather than know it, `[nobody]` when it is deliberately unresolved. Few and deep: three to five facts per question, each concrete enough to speak aloud at a table. Check every coined name against the rules vocabulary (Grade, Beat, Zone, Sector, Domain, Principle, Mandate, Trial, Accord, Aura, Rift, Imprint, Temper) and against the names already in the setting file.*

## 1. What Aether smells and sounds like

**What the book has.** Two anchors: Volatile Energy leaves the dying as pale motes, and a human's first contact with Aether is intoxicating. The setting file's Sensory direction covers what Consolidation, a Breakthrough, Aura Pressure, a Principle, and a Domain feel like from inside. The System's visible presence is fine cyan geometry. Nothing yet says what Aether smells like or sounds like.

**The fact that constrains the answer.** Aether and VE are one substance in two states. Aether is yours; VE is Aether still wearing the shape of whatever held it last. So VE released at a death can carry a trace of the thing that died, and that trace is a discoverable fact, which is how the System reads residue and why it wants what a death releases.

**What is needed.** Smell and sound at three moments, each as an observable a character could report without a vocabulary for it:

- Ambient, in a high-density region, where Consolidation refines 40 VE an hour instead of 20.
- During Consolidation, as VE refines into Aether inside the body.
- At a death, as the motes leave, and how the trace of what died shows in smell or sound.

Also: whether an ordinary Level 1 human perceives any of this on Day 1, and what sharpens it (Grade, Perception, or time). One line.

**Used in.** The first-Aether vignette (a lore box in Cultivation), the tutorial's Consolidation narration, the read-aloud for a death, and the companion app's ambient text. Read-aloud stays inside what the character can perceive, so every fact must be sayable in a sentence that describes a sensation, never an adjective about the air.

## 2. The Kith after the tutorial

**What the book has.** Four Kith (Kes the scout, Nemi with the Greater Healing Pill, Sava with two shards, Tovan who wants to fight) spent nineteen days in the tutorial sector and exit the gate beside the party, onto Earth, late on Day 3, with Interpretation working between the two species for the first time. Kes tallied forty-one living things falling from the sky and watched most of them die in their first hour; Kes also watched what each party member did on the slopes. The Kith are from Oren, an old Integrated world; their route into a human tutorial is `[nobody]`. Established worlds are barred from entering Earth during accession, and the Kith are tutorial returnees whose gate delivered them to Earth rather than home. Kith judge particular humans by experience, and none of the four speaks for their people.

**What is needed.**

- What each of the four believes about humans on Day 3, from what they watched. One line each, in character, and different from one another.
- What each wants in the first week on Earth. Kes wants an onward route and a share in a traveling workshop; Sava is drawn to human tools and may stay; Tovan resents a shrinking range of choices; Nemi is cautious. Sharpen these into something a GM can play in a scene: a request, a refusal, a trade.
- Whether they can get home and how. A System return authorization they may accept or decline is the working assumption; say when it is offered, what it costs, and what happens to one who declines it.
- How word of humans reaches Oren: through a returning Kith, through Open Measure's bulletins, through the Wayhouse Compact, or through a household's travel account. Three facts, `[GM]`.
- What an Oren household does when a member comes back from a human tutorial. One line.

**Used in.** The Kith section of the After the Gate chapter, the Stinger in which one Kith says the first translated sentence, and any later Oren content. Constraint: no species-wide verdict on humans, and no Kith who exists to teach the party a lesson.

## 3. The in-world sources

**What the book has.** Five recurring in-world documents are proposed as the sources for chapter epigraphs, lore boxes, and asides, so that the whole book reads as edited from a world's records rather than written cold: the Gethra Accession Ledger, Open Measure bulletins, the survivors' forum, Bel Sar's route accounts, and the System's own notices. The setting file gives each a one-line voice and the one way it is allowed to be wrong.

**The rule that constrains the answer.** The sourcebook gives the GM reliable information. An epigraph is true by default. A wrong one appears only where the chapter's GM text names the error, and the error is always one of the System's adopted quirks or an ordinary human mistake, never a rule stated wrongly.

**What is needed, for each of the five.**

- Who writes it, who reads it, and how it physically exists: a copied codex with disagreeing copies, a bulletin posted at Wayhouses, a forum on surviving networks relayed by radio and paper, a route account in a traveler's hand, System notices in the interface.
- Its voice in three words, and one sample sentence in that voice that states a true fact from the setting file.
- What it characteristically gets wrong, and how a GM can tell from the text alone.
- For the Ledger: how old it is in the setting's own terms, and which schools hold copies.
- For Open Measure: what a bulletin's header looks like (conditions, sample size, byline).
- For the forum: what it is called by the people on it, and where a GM says it lives.

Also: whether a sixth source earns a place. Candidates: a Wayhouse ledger, a Mael Torr contract, a Pellam route account. Argue for one or none.

**Used in.** The epigraph brief (`lore/briefs/epigraphs-prompt.md`), the found logs, the survivors' forum handout later, and the Initiate's Manual's planted errors.
